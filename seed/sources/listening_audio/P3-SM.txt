Designing a Survey for a Sociology Assignment

[0:00] Professor: So, Fatima, Ben, your survey on remote working. How many people are you planning to ask?

[0:05] Speaker 2: About 30?

[0:06] Professor: That's too few for meaningful percentages. You need 50 at the very least.

[0:09] Speaker 3: For the questions, we were going to make them all closed, multiple choice.

[0:13] Professor: That's easier to analyze, but add a handful of open questions so people can explain in their own words.

[0:18] Speaker 2: I'm worried about who we're asking. Nearly everyone we know works in an office, so our sample might end up rather narrow.

[0:24] Professor: Then approach a community group. You'll reach shop workers, nurses, retired people.

[0:26] Speaker 3: Should we give people something for their time?

[0:28] Professor: What did you have in mind?

[0:29] Speaker 3: A prize draw seems complicated, so maybe a small voucher?

[0:33] Professor: That's fine.

[0:34] Speaker 2: How long should it take? 20 minutes?

[0:36] Professor: Half that at the most. After 10 minutes, people start giving up.

[0:38] Professor: And don't open with the questions about income. People find those sensitive. Put them towards the end.

[0:42] Speaker 3: We were going to try it out on five friends first.

[0:45] Professor: Make it 10, and not just friends. The main reason for a pilot is to catch any confusing wording.

[0:49] Speaker 2: Should we do it on paper?

[0:51] Professor: Use an online survey tool. The department has a license.

[0:54] Professor: The biggest mistake I see is questions that push people towards a particular answer. Avoid those.

[0:58] Speaker 2: I'll work through the closed answers using statistical software.

[1:42] Speaker 3: And I'll code the open answers into thematic categories.

[1:45] Professor: Before any of the questions, you'll need a short statement asking for participants' consent.

[1:49] Speaker 2: How long can we keep the data?

[1:51] Professor: Personal data must be deleted after a year at the most. 12 months.

[1:54] Professor: And the survey has to be approved by the ethics committee before it goes out. My approval isn't enough.

[1:58] Speaker 3: How will we report the results?

[2:00] Professor: Not as a written handout. You'll give a short presentation in class.

[2:03] Professor: And I'd like a draft of your methodology section by Friday.

[2:05] Speaker 2: And the word limit?

[2:07] Professor: 2,500 with the final submission at the end of term.

[2:10] Speaker 3: Thanks. That's really helpful.
