A Seminar Discussion on Assessing a Marketing Campaign_part3

[0:00] Interviewer: So, your marketing campaign assignment. Whose campaign did you choose?

[0:02] Grace: We almost went with Brightfit, but there wasn't enough data, so we chose Northline.

[0:06] Interviewer: Northline makes a lot of things.

[0:08] Student: Right. Smart watches, headphones, but this campaign was just for their fitness app.

[0:14] Interviewer: Which channels did they use?

[0:15] Grace: They ran ads on TikTok and YouTube, but Instagram got by far the most likes and shares.

[0:20] Interviewer: And the impact?

[0:21] Student: Downloads went up 20%, but the more important figure is sign-ups.

[0:24] Student: They rose 35%.

[0:26] Interviewer: What did it all cost?

[0:27] Grace: They spent 40,000 on social media alone, but the overall figure was $50,000.

[0:32] Interviewer: Who was the target audience?

[0:33] Student: They aimed at 25- to 34-year-olds, but the group that actually responded best was 18 to 24.

[0:38] Interviewer: Grace, where would you start the analysis?

[0:40] Grace: Before looking at sign-ups, I'd look at the click-through rate, whether people even clicked on the ads.

[0:44] Interviewer: Good. Students always measure new customers and forget about customer retention, whether people stay with the product.

[0:51] Student: We've hardly mentioned that.

[0:52] Interviewer: How did Northline get feedback?

[0:53] Grace: They didn't use focus groups, just an online survey emailed to everyone who joined.

[0:57] Student: 72% were positive, and only about 10% were negative.

[1:00] Interviewer: What did people complain about?

[1:02] Grace: A few mentioned bugs, but mostly it was the price. People felt the subscription was too expensive.

[1:06] Interviewer: Are you comparing it with a rival?

[1:08] Student: We looked at FitPulse, but it's tiny. FitTrack is their main competitor.

[1:11] Interviewer: How are you crunching the numbers? A statistics package?

[1:13] Grace: We thought about one, but a spreadsheet does everything we need.

[1:15] Interviewer: How have you divided the work?

[1:16] Student: Grace has done the data, and I'm writing the recommendation section.

[1:19] Interviewer: Grace, what's the key change you suggest?

[1:21] Grace: The advertising is working. It's the pricing model that needs to change. Maybe a cheaper tier.

[1:25] Interviewer: How long did the campaign run?

[1:27] Student: It was planned for a month, but they extended it. Six weeks in the end.

[1:29] Interviewer: Which channel did worst?

[1:30] Grace: Radio was poor, but the print adverts gave the lowest return on investment.

[1:34] Interviewer: In your conclusion, don't just celebrate the jump in sign-ups. Focus on the long-term impact.

[1:38] Interviewer: When is it due?

[1:39] Student: We thought Friday, but it's next Monday.

[1:40] Interviewer: And keep it to 2,000 words. Don't go over.

[1:43] Grace: We're at about 2,500, so we'll cut it down.
