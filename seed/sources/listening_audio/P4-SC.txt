How Birds Navigate During Migration

[0:00] Lecturer: Good afternoon. Today's lecture is about how birds find their way during migration, a topic that has puzzled scientists for a very long time.

[0:05] Lecturer: And one where we're still discovering new details. Let's start with timing.

[0:10] Lecturer: Many songbirds migrate at night, which might seem surprising given the obvious risks of flying in darkness. There's actually a good reason for this.

[0:16] Lecturer: Flying in the dark helps birds to avoid predators, many of which, like hawks, hunt by sight during daylight hours and pose far less threat once the sun has gone down.

[0:26] Lecturer: Of course, not all migration happens at night.

[0:28] Lecturer: In daylight, many birds use the sun as a compass, orienting their flight direction relative to its position in the sky.

[0:42] Lecturer: For birds that fly at night, a different celestial cue takes over.

[0:45] Lecturer: Birds that fly at night can learn the pattern of the stars, using the night sky much as sailors once did for navigation.

[0:52] Lecturer: This isn't something birds are simply born knowing in full.

[0:54] Lecturer: Experiments have shown that young birds learn to find the point around which the night sky rotates, essentially learning the position of what we'd call the pole star by watching the sky as chicks.

[1:21] Lecturer: An older theory proposed something different.

[1:49] Lecturer: Now, here's a fascinating point about how migratory direction develops in young birds.
