Discussing a Research Proposal on Urban Bee Populations

[0:00] Interviewer: So, Wei, tell me where the proposal has got to.

[0:03] Student: Hmm. Well, I started out wanting to compare bees in the countryside and in towns. But I've narrowed it down. I'm now only looking at urban areas.

[0:13] Interviewer: Sensible. And your central question?

[0:14] Student: Climate and habitat loss are obviously important. But what I really want to find out is whether pesticides, especially the chemicals people buy for their gardens, are reducing bee numbers.

[0:24] Interviewer: Where will you be collecting data?

[0:25] Student: I'll compare parks with gardens, public green space against private.

[0:29] Interviewer: Something else that will affect numbers is how many kinds of flower there are at each site. I'd measure flower diversity as well, or you won't be able to separate the effects.

[0:35] Student: I hadn't thought of that.

[0:36] Interviewer: How long will you be collecting data for?

[0:38] Student: I'd thought about six months over the summer, but bees behave so differently through the year that I've gone for a 12-month period.

[0:45] Interviewer: Because you'll be on council land, you'll need sign off from the ethics committee before you start, not just permission from the council.

[0:52] Student: The application's half written.

[0:53] Interviewer: And your main method?

[0:54] Student: I considered traps, but I don't want to kill any bees. So, I'll do timed bee counts. I stand at a fixed point for 10 minutes and count every bee that lands.

[1:02] Interviewer: Anything qualitative?

[1:03] Student: I'm going to talk to local beekeepers. Some of them have been watching their hives for decades.

[1:08] Interviewer: For the counts, follow a standard survey protocol so that other people can compare their results with yours.

[1:13] Student: I'll find out which one is most widely used.

[1:15] Interviewer: One big risk is the weather. On a cold or windy day, bee activity drops sharply, so record the conditions on every visit.

[1:22] Student: Temperature and wind speed then.

[1:24] Interviewer: And keep a detailed field notebook, not just the numbers, but anything unusual you notice.

[1:28] Student: I hadn't really thought about funding.

[1:30] Interviewer: The department offers a small research grant, and the deadline is coming up soon.

[1:34] Interviewer: Will you be sharing any findings before the end?

[1:44] Student: Okay.

[1:45] Interviewer: One weakness I often see in early drafts is claims that are far too broad. Six sites in one city can't tell you about bees across the whole country.

[1:53] Student: I'll be careful.

[1:54] Interviewer: When is the full proposal due?

[1:55] Student: Well, it was originally the end of the month, but the department has extended it to the end of term.
