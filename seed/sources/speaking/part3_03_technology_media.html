1. Social Media

The role and impact of social networking platforms on daily life.

Q1Opinion
Do you think social media has a positive or negative effect on society?

I think it is genuinely a mixed blessing. On the positive side, social media connects people across distances, gives a voice to the marginalised and spreads information rapidly. However, its negative effects are equally significant, from spreading misinformation to fuelling anxiety and comparison. In my view, the platforms themselves are neutral; the real issue is how we use them. Used mindfully, the benefits can outweigh the drawbacks.

Vocabulary: neutral · mindfully · drawbacks

Q2Comparison
How has social media changed the way people communicate?

It has transformed communication dramatically. In the past, staying in touch required letters or calls, whereas now we can share our lives instantly with hundreds of people at once. Communication has become faster and more visual, but arguably more superficial. We interact more frequently yet often less meaningfully, replacing deep conversations with quick likes and comments. So it has widened our reach while sometimes shallowing our connections.

Vocabulary: visual · superficial · meaningfully

Q3Social Impact
How does social media affect young people's mental health?

The impact can be quite concerning. Constant exposure to carefully curated, idealised images can fuel feelings of inadequacy and low self-esteem, particularly regarding appearance. The pressure to gain likes and the fear of missing out can also cause real anxiety. That said, it can also provide community and support. On balance, though, I believe excessive use is genuinely detrimental to young people's wellbeing.

Vocabulary: fuel · detrimental · wellbeing

Q4Cause & Effect
Why do you think people share so much of their lives online?

I think it stems largely from a deep human desire for validation and connection. Receiving likes and comments triggers a sense of approval that can be quite addictive. Many people also enjoy documenting their lives and keeping in touch. However, this compulsion to share can tip into oversharing, where people curate an idealised image, sometimes at the expense of actually living in the moment.

Vocabulary: addictive · compulsion · curate

Q5Future
How do you think social media will develop in the future?

I anticipate it will become even more immersive, possibly through virtual and augmented reality, blurring the line between online and offline life further. Artificial intelligence will make content ever more personalised, which raises concerns about echo chambers. Hopefully, there will also be a growing backlash leading to more regulation and healthier usage habits. So it will likely become more sophisticated, but not necessarily more beneficial.

Vocabulary: personalised · sophisticated · beneficial

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to reduce the harmful effects of social media?

A multi-pronged approach is needed. On an individual level, promoting digital literacy and encouraging conscious limits on usage helps enormously. Platforms themselves should be held accountable for their algorithms and do more to combat misinformation and protect young users. Governments may also need to introduce sensible regulation. Ultimately, teaching people to engage critically rather than passively is the most sustainable solution.

Vocabulary: accountable · regulation · critically

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of social media for businesses?

For businesses, the advantages are substantial: it offers a cheap, powerful way to reach a huge audience, build a brand and engage directly with customers. Small businesses in particular can thrive. The disadvantages include the risk of negative publicity spreading rapidly, the pressure to constantly produce content, and vulnerability to online backlash. So it is a powerful tool, but one that requires careful, strategic management.

Vocabulary: thrive · backlash · strategic

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say social media does more harm than good. Do you agree?

I am somewhat torn on this. There is compelling evidence of harm, from mental health issues to the spread of misinformation, which I take seriously. However, I cannot ignore the genuine benefits, such as connecting isolated people and enabling social movements. So rather than declaring it simply harmful, I would say its impact is highly dependent on usage. The technology is not inherently malign; our habits determine its net effect.

Vocabulary: dependent · malign · net

2. Artificial Intelligence

The rise of AI and its effects on work, life, and society.

Q1Opinion
Do you think artificial intelligence will benefit or harm humanity?

I believe AI has the potential to do both, and the outcome depends entirely on how we manage it. On the positive side, it could revolutionise medicine, science and productivity, solving problems beyond human capacity. On the other hand, it raises serious concerns about job displacement, privacy and even safety. So I am cautiously optimistic, but I firmly believe it requires careful ethical oversight to remain beneficial rather than harmful.

Vocabulary: ethical · beneficial · harmful

Q2Comparison
Are there things humans can do that AI never will?

I firmly believe so, at least for the foreseeable future. While AI excels at processing data and pattern recognition, it lacks genuine consciousness, emotional depth and lived experience. True creativity, empathy, moral judgement and the ability to understand context in a human way remain uniquely ours. AI can mimic these, but I do not think it can authentically replicate the human essence behind them.

Vocabulary: mimic · replicate · essence

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect will AI have on employment?

The effect will be profound and disruptive. AI will inevitably automate many jobs, particularly routine and repetitive ones, which will displace some workers. However, history suggests it will also create new roles and industries we cannot yet imagine. The key challenge is managing the transition, ensuring that displaced workers can retrain. Without such support, there is a genuine risk of increased inequality and social disruption.

Vocabulary: automate · transition · inequality

Q4Social Impact
How is AI already changing everyday life?

AI is already deeply embedded in our daily routines, often invisibly. It powers the recommendations on our streaming services, the navigation on our phones, and the virtual assistants we speak to. It filters our emails and even helps diagnose illnesses. Most of this makes life more convenient and efficient. However, this quiet integration also raises important questions about how much of our lives we are handing over to algorithms.

Vocabulary: embedded · efficient · integration

Q5Future
Should there be limits on the development of AI?

Yes, I strongly believe sensible limits and regulation are essential. The technology is advancing so rapidly that it risks outpacing our ability to understand its consequences. Without ethical guidelines and oversight, there are real dangers regarding privacy, bias and even autonomous weapons. This does not mean stifling innovation, but rather guiding it responsibly. I think proactive governance is far wiser than trying to contain the risks after they emerge.

Vocabulary: stifling · governance · contain

Q6Problem & Solution
How can societies prepare for the widespread use of AI?

Preparation must happen on several fronts. Education systems need to adapt, focusing on the creative and interpersonal skills that AI cannot replicate, while also teaching digital literacy. Governments should invest in retraining programmes for workers whose jobs are at risk. Crucially, robust ethical frameworks and regulations must be developed proactively. A social safety net may also be needed to cushion those affected by the disruption.

Vocabulary: ethical · cushion · disruption

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the main benefits and risks of using AI in healthcare?

The benefits are enormous. AI can analyse medical images with incredible accuracy, help diagnose diseases early, and accelerate drug discovery, potentially saving countless lives. However, the risks include the danger of algorithmic bias leading to unequal care, concerns over patient data privacy, and the potential for errors if AI is trusted blindly. So while it is a genuinely powerful tool, human oversight remains absolutely indispensable.

Vocabulary: blindly · tool · indispensable

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some fear AI will one day become smarter than humans. Do you share this concern?

I have measured concern rather than alarm. AI already surpasses us in narrow tasks like calculation, and it will keep improving. The idea of a general intelligence exceeding humans is more speculative, but not impossible in the long term. My concern is less about a science-fiction takeover and more about the immediate risks of misuse and lack of control. So I think a degree of vigilance is wise, but hysteria is unhelpful.

Vocabulary: speculative · vigilance · hysteria

3. Digital Communication

Messaging, video calls, and how technology shapes the way we connect.

Q1Opinion
Do you think digital communication is better than face-to-face communication?

I would say it is not better, but rather different, with its own strengths. Digital communication offers unmatched convenience and lets us stay connected across any distance instantly. However, it lacks the richness of face-to-face interaction, where tone, body language and physical presence convey so much. For deep, emotional conversations, nothing beats being together in person. So digital tools are a wonderful supplement, but not a full substitute for genuine human contact.

Vocabulary: supplement · substitute · contact

Q2Comparison
How is the way young people communicate different from older generations?

The difference is quite marked. Young people have grown up as digital natives, so they favour instant messaging, voice notes and social media over phone calls, which many find intrusive. Older generations often prefer calls or in-person conversation. Young people also communicate more through images, emojis and memes, a kind of visual shorthand. This can lead to occasional misunderstanding between the generations, though both are simply adapting to their era.

Vocabulary: visual · misunderstanding · adapting

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect has instant messaging had on our language?

It has had a noticeable influence. Instant messaging has spawned a whole new informal language of abbreviations, emojis and slang, which allows for quick, expressive communication. Some worry this is eroding traditional writing skills and grammar, especially among the young. However, I take a more relaxed view; language has always evolved, and people generally understand when to switch between casual and formal registers. So it is evolution, not decay.

Vocabulary: eroding · evolved · registers

Q4Social Impact
Has technology made us more or less connected to each other?

This is a fascinating paradox. On the surface, technology has made us more connected than ever, allowing instant contact with anyone worldwide. Yet many argue we are actually more isolated, replacing deep, present relationships with shallow digital interactions. We may have hundreds of online contacts but few genuine confidants. So I would say we are more connected in quantity but perhaps less connected in quality and true intimacy.

Vocabulary: connected · quality · intimacy

Q5Future
How do you think we will communicate in the future?

I imagine communication will become even more immersive and instantaneous. Technologies like virtual reality could allow us to feel genuinely present with distant people, and real-time translation may dissolve language barriers entirely. We may increasingly communicate through augmented reality overlays too. While exciting, I hope these advances enhance rather than replace human warmth, and that we do not lose the value of simple, unmediated face-to-face connection.

Vocabulary: enhance · unmediated · connection

Q6Problem & Solution
How can people avoid becoming too dependent on digital communication?

Being intentional is key. Setting aside device-free time, particularly during meals or when with loved ones, helps enormously. Consciously choosing to call or meet someone rather than always texting preserves deeper connection. Turning off unnecessary notifications reduces the constant pull of our devices. Essentially, we need to treat technology as a tool that serves us, rather than allowing it to dominate our attention and displace real interaction.

Vocabulary: dominate · displace · interaction

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of communicating mainly through text?

Text communication is convenient, allowing you to reply at your own pace and keep a written record, which is useful. It is also less intrusive than a call. However, the major disadvantage is the absence of tone and body language, which makes messages easy to misinterpret. It can also feel impersonal and lead to misunderstandings that a quick conversation would avoid. So it is efficient, but emotionally limited.

Vocabulary: misinterpret · impersonal · limited

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say technology is making face-to-face conversation a dying skill. Do you agree?

I think there is a kernel of truth to this, though I would not go so far as to call it dying. It is certainly true that some young people, accustomed to texting, can feel awkward in direct conversation and may struggle to read social cues. However, humans are inherently social, and the need for real connection persists. So the skill may be underused and in need of nurturing, but I do not believe it will ever become obsolete.

Vocabulary: underused · nurturing · obsolete

4. Online Privacy

Data protection, surveillance, and privacy in the digital age.

Q1Opinion
How important is online privacy to you?

I consider online privacy extremely important, though I admit I do not always protect it as well as I should. In an age where our data is constantly collected, I believe people have a fundamental right to control their personal information. The erosion of privacy can lead to manipulation and surveillance, which is genuinely worrying. So while convenience often tempts us to be careless, I think safeguarding our privacy is a vital and overlooked responsibility.

Vocabulary: vital · overlooked · responsibility

Q2Comparison
Do people care about privacy more or less than they used to?

This is quite paradoxical. On one hand, awareness of privacy issues has grown enormously following major data scandals, so people claim to care more. On the other hand, our actual behaviour suggests otherwise; we readily hand over data for the sake of convenience and free services. This gap between concern and action is often called the privacy paradox. So people are more aware, yet often equally willing to compromise their privacy.

Vocabulary: aware · willing · compromise

Q3Cause & Effect
What are the consequences of companies collecting so much personal data?

The consequences are significant and often hidden. Companies use our data to target advertising with uncanny precision, which can feel manipulative. More seriously, large data collections are vulnerable to breaches, potentially exposing people to fraud and identity theft. There is also the broader concern of this information being used to influence our behaviour and opinions. Essentially, it creates a huge imbalance of power that can undermine individual autonomy.

Vocabulary: influence · imbalance · undermine

Q4Social Impact
How does the loss of privacy affect society?

A society without privacy can become one of pervasive surveillance, which can have a chilling effect on free expression. If people feel constantly watched, they may self-censor and conform, which is unhealthy for democracy. The concentration of personal data in a few powerful hands also creates opportunities for manipulation on a mass scale. So the erosion of privacy is not merely a personal issue; it has profound implications for freedom itself.

Vocabulary: manipulation · implications · freedom

Q5Future
Do you think privacy will exist in the future?

I am somewhat pessimistic, though not entirely without hope. As technology becomes ever more pervasive, with smart devices and surveillance everywhere, maintaining privacy will become increasingly difficult. However, I am hopeful that growing public awareness and stronger regulations, like data protection laws, could push back against this trend. So while privacy as we once knew it may diminish, I believe the fight to preserve a meaningful degree of it will intensify.

Vocabulary: pervasive · preserve · intensify

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to better protect people's online privacy?

Solutions must operate at multiple levels. Governments should enforce robust data protection laws that give people genuine control over their information, like Europe's regulations. Companies must be more transparent about how they use data. On an individual level, education about privacy settings, strong passwords and cautious sharing is vital. Ultimately, shifting the default towards protecting the user, rather than exploiting them, is the most important safeguard.

Vocabulary: transparent · exploiting · safeguard

Q7Adv & Disadv
Are there any benefits to sharing personal data with companies?

Yes, there are genuine trade-offs. Sharing data allows companies to offer highly personalised and convenient services, from tailored recommendations to smoother experiences. Many valuable free services are funded this way. In healthcare, shared data can even advance medical research. The problem is the lack of transparency and consent. So the benefits are real, but they should come from an informed, voluntary exchange rather than covert exploitation.

Vocabulary: transparency · voluntary · exploitation

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say if you have nothing to hide, privacy does not matter. Do you agree?

I strongly disagree with that argument. Privacy is not about hiding wrongdoing; it is a fundamental right that protects our autonomy and dignity. We all have private thoughts and information that are simply nobody else's business, regardless of innocence. This argument also dangerously assumes those in power will always act benevolently. In my view, defending privacy is about protecting freedom itself, not concealing anything sinister.

Vocabulary: freedom · concealing · sinister

5. Media & News

How news is produced and consumed, and media influence on opinion.

Q1Opinion
How important is it for people to follow the news?

I believe it is quite important for people to stay reasonably informed, as it is part of being an engaged citizen. Understanding current events helps us make sense of the world and participate in democracy. However, I would add a caveat; consuming too much news, especially negative or sensational content, can be overwhelming and anxiety-inducing. So the ideal is to stay informed through reliable sources without becoming obsessed.

Vocabulary: caveat · overwhelming · reliable

Q2Comparison
How has the way people get news changed over the years?

The change has been revolutionary. In the past, people relied on scheduled TV bulletins and printed newspapers, receiving curated news once or twice a day. Now, news is constant, instant and consumed largely through social media and online sources. This has made news more accessible, but also more fragmented and harder to verify. The rise of citizen journalism is a benefit, but so too is the spread of unverified, sensational content.

Vocabulary: fragmented · unverified · sensational

Q3Cause & Effect
Why do you think fake news spreads so easily?

Several factors are at play. Social media algorithms tend to prioritise engaging content, and false or sensational stories are often more provocative than the truth. People also share things impulsively without verifying them, especially if they confirm existing beliefs. The lack of traditional editorial gatekeepers online means misinformation can spread unchecked. Combined with a general decline in media literacy, this creates the perfect conditions for falsehoods to proliferate.

Vocabulary: provocative · unchecked · proliferate

Q4Social Impact
How does the media influence public opinion?

The media wields enormous influence over public opinion. By choosing which stories to cover and how to frame them, it shapes what people think is important and how they interpret events. This agenda-setting power is significant. Repeated exposure to particular viewpoints can gradually shape attitudes, sometimes reinforcing bias. So while a free media is vital for democracy, its power to sway opinion also carries real responsibility.

Vocabulary: shape · sway · responsibility

Q5Future
Do you think traditional newspapers will survive in the future?

In their printed form, I think they will largely decline, as younger generations consume news almost entirely online. However, I do not believe quality journalism itself will disappear; rather, it will continue its shift to digital platforms and subscription models. The reputable news organisations behind the papers may well endure, just in a different format. So the printed page may fade, but the institution of journalism should persist.

Vocabulary: endure · institution · persist

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to combat the spread of misinformation?

Tackling this requires a combined effort. Improving media literacy so people can critically evaluate sources is perhaps the most important long-term solution. Social media platforms must also take greater responsibility, using fact-checking and adjusting algorithms that reward sensationalism. Supporting quality, independent journalism is equally vital. Ultimately, an informed, sceptical public is the best defence against the tide of misinformation.

Vocabulary: evaluate · informed · defence

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of having 24-hour news?

The advantage is that we are kept constantly informed of breaking events as they unfold, which can be valuable in a crisis. However, the disadvantages are considerable. The pressure to fill airtime often leads to speculation, repetition and sensationalism over substance. It can also foster a state of perpetual anxiety and desensitise us to serious events. So constant news offers immediacy at the cost of depth and perspective.

Vocabulary: sensationalism · desensitise · perspective

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say the media focuses too much on negative news. Do you agree?

I largely agree with this. There is a well-known principle in journalism that bad news sells, so outlets tend to prioritise disasters, conflict and scandal, which are more attention-grabbing. This can give people a distorted, overly pessimistic view of the world, ignoring genuine progress. While reporting problems is important, this relentless negativity can be demoralising. A more balanced approach, including constructive news, would be healthier.

Vocabulary: pessimistic · negativity · demoralising

6. Smartphones & Devices

The role of smartphones in modern life and screen dependence.

Q1Opinion
Do you think smartphones have improved people's lives?

On the whole, I think they have, though not without significant drawbacks. Smartphones have put a world of information, communication and services in our pockets, which is genuinely empowering and convenient. However, they have also introduced constant distraction and a degree of dependence that can harm our focus and relationships. So I see them as a remarkable tool that has improved life, provided we use them consciously rather than compulsively.

Vocabulary: dependence · consciously · compulsively

Q2Comparison
How was life different before smartphones?

Life was noticeably slower and, in some ways, more present. People were less contactable and more likely to be fully engaged in their immediate surroundings rather than staring at a screen. Getting lost, being bored or having to memorise information were normal. While we have gained incredible convenience, I think we have lost a certain patience and the ability to simply be idle, which had its own value.

Vocabulary: patience · idle · value

Q3Cause & Effect
What effects does excessive smartphone use have on people?

The effects can be quite detrimental. Excessive use is linked to reduced attention spans, disrupted sleep and increased anxiety, partly due to the constant stream of notifications. It can also erode face-to-face social skills and lead to a sedentary lifestyle. There is even a recognised phenomenon of phone addiction. So while occasional use is harmless, overuse can genuinely impair both our mental and physical wellbeing.

Vocabulary: addiction · impair · wellbeing

Q4Social Impact
How have smartphones changed social interactions?

They have transformed them in mixed ways. On one hand, they let us maintain connections effortlessly across distances. On the other, they often intrude on our real-world interactions; it is common to see people together physically but absorbed in their phones. This phenomenon, sometimes called phubbing, can make people feel ignored. So smartphones have expanded our virtual social lives while sometimes diminishing the quality of our present ones.

Vocabulary: intrude · ignored · diminishing

Q5Future
How do you think mobile technology will develop in the future?

I anticipate devices will become even more integrated into our lives, perhaps evolving beyond the handheld phone into wearables or augmented reality glasses. Artificial intelligence will make them ever more anticipatory and personalised. The line between the digital and physical worlds is likely to blur further. While this promises even greater convenience, I hope it comes with a stronger awareness of the need to remain present and in control.

Vocabulary: convenience · present · control

Q6Problem & Solution
How can people reduce their dependence on their phones?

There are several practical strategies. Turning off non-essential notifications immediately reduces the constant pull to check the phone. Designating device-free times and zones, such as during meals or in the bedroom, helps re-establish boundaries. Using apps that track and limit screen time can raise awareness of usage. Ultimately, consciously replacing mindless scrolling with more rewarding activities is the most sustainable way to loosen the phone's grip.

Vocabulary: replacing · rewarding · grip

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of children owning smartphones?

The advantages include safety, as parents can stay in contact, and access to educational resources. It also helps children develop digital literacy. However, the disadvantages are considerable: exposure to inappropriate content, cyberbullying, addiction and reduced physical activity. It can also hinder the development of face-to-face social skills. So while there are benefits, I believe children's phone use needs careful supervision and sensible limits.

Vocabulary: hinder · supervision · limits

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say people cannot live without their phones now. Do you agree?

I think that is an exaggeration, but it contains an uncomfortable truth. Obviously, people survived perfectly well before phones, so it is not a literal dependency. However, phones have become so integral to how we work, navigate and communicate that living without one would be genuinely inconvenient and isolating in modern society. So while we could live without them, our society has become so reliant on them that it would be a difficult adjustment.

Vocabulary: integral · inconvenient · reliant

7. The Internet & Information

Access to information, the digital divide, and online reliability.

Q1Opinion
Do you think the internet has been a good thing for society?

Overwhelmingly, yes, though it is not without serious problems. The internet has democratised access to information and education, connected the world, and driven economic innovation in extraordinary ways. It has empowered individuals and given voice to the voiceless. However, it has also enabled misinformation, cybercrime and privacy violations. On balance, I consider it one of humanity's most transformative and beneficial inventions, provided we manage its downsides responsibly.

Vocabulary: transformative · downsides · responsibly

Q2Comparison
How did people find information before the internet?

It was a far more laborious process. People relied on libraries, encyclopaedias, newspapers and asking knowledgeable individuals. Finding a specific fact could take hours or even days of research. While this was slow, some argue it encouraged deeper engagement and better retention. Today, information is instant, but arguably we process it more superficially. So the internet has traded depth and effort for unprecedented speed and convenience.

Vocabulary: superficially · depth · speed

Q3Cause & Effect
What are the effects of having so much information available online?

The effects are double-edged. On the positive side, unprecedented access to information empowers learning and informed decision-making. However, this sheer abundance can lead to information overload, making it hard to focus or discern what is reliable. It can also create echo chambers, where algorithms feed us only what we already believe. So while access is empowering, navigating it wisely requires strong discernment to avoid being overwhelmed or misled.

Vocabulary: discernment · overwhelmed · misled

Q4Social Impact
How has the internet affected inequality between people?

It has had a complex effect. In some ways it has reduced inequality by giving people everywhere access to free education and economic opportunities. However, it has also created a new form of inequality, the so-called digital divide, between those with reliable access and skills and those without. Those lacking connectivity or literacy risk being left further behind. So the internet is a double-edged force that can either narrow or widen disparities.

Vocabulary: behind · double-edged · disparities

Q5Future
Do you think everyone will have internet access in the future?

I am hopeful that access will become nearly universal, though significant gaps will likely persist for some time. Technology like satellite internet is rapidly extending connectivity to remote regions, and access is increasingly seen as a basic necessity. However, cost and infrastructure remain real barriers in poorer areas. So while I believe the digital divide will narrow considerably, achieving truly universal access will require sustained global investment.

Vocabulary: narrow · universal · investment

Q6Problem & Solution
How can people learn to tell reliable information from unreliable information online?

Developing critical thinking is essential. People should learn to check the credibility of a source, look for corroboration from multiple reputable outlets, and be wary of sensational headlines designed to provoke. Being aware of one's own biases is also important. Ideally, these media literacy skills should be taught in schools from a young age, equipping people to scrutinise information rather than accepting it uncritically.

Vocabulary: equipping · scrutinise · uncritically

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of children using the internet for learning?

The advantages are substantial: the internet offers a vast library of educational content, interactive tools and access to knowledge that enriches learning enormously. It caters to different learning styles too. The disadvantages include exposure to unreliable or inappropriate content, distraction, and the risk of reduced deep reading. So it is a phenomenal educational resource, but children need guidance and supervision to use it safely and effectively.

Vocabulary: guidance · supervision · safely

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say the internet makes people less intelligent. Do you agree?

I disagree with that as a blanket statement, though it raises a fair concern. The internet does not make us less intelligent; rather, it changes how we use our intelligence. We may memorise less, relying on it as an external memory, but we have unparalleled access to knowledge. The risk is that passive consumption and short attention spans can weaken deep thinking. So it is a tool that can either enrich or diminish the mind, depending on use.

Vocabulary: passive · enrich · diminish

8. Online Entertainment

Streaming, gaming, and the shift in how people are entertained.

Q1Opinion
Do you think online entertainment is better than traditional forms?

I would say it is not necessarily better, but far more convenient and personalised. Streaming and online platforms offer endless choice on demand, which is hard to beat. However, traditional forms like cinema, live theatre or reading a physical book offer a certain quality and communal experience that online consumption can lack. So I appreciate the convenience, but I do not think it fully replaces the richness of certain traditional experiences.

Vocabulary: replaces · richness · experiences

Q2Comparison
How is the way people are entertained today different from the past?

The difference is stark. In the past, entertainment was largely communal and scheduled; families gathered to watch a programme at a set time, or went out to the cinema. Today, entertainment is highly individualised and on-demand, consumed alone on personal devices whenever we wish. This offers wonderful freedom, but I think we have lost some of the shared, communal aspect. Entertainment has become more personalised yet arguably more solitary.

Vocabulary: communal · personalised · solitary

Q3Cause & Effect
Why has streaming become so popular?

Its popularity comes down to convenience and choice. Streaming offers instant, on-demand access to an enormous library of content that you can watch anywhere, anytime, without adverts. It is also relatively affordable compared to traditional cable. The personalised recommendations keep people engaged, and binge-watching entire series has become a cultural phenomenon. Essentially, it perfectly suits the modern desire for flexibility and instant gratification.

Vocabulary: engaged · flexibility · instant

Q4Social Impact
What impact do video games have on young people?

The impact is genuinely mixed and often exaggerated. On the positive side, many games improve problem-solving, coordination and even teamwork when played socially, and they are a legitimate form of relaxation. However, excessive gaming can lead to addiction, a sedentary lifestyle and, in some cases, exposure to toxic online behaviour. So like most things, video games are beneficial in moderation but harmful to excess.

Vocabulary: toxic · beneficial · harmful

Q5Future
How do you think entertainment will change in the future?

I expect entertainment to become far more immersive and interactive. Virtual and augmented reality could allow us to step inside stories and games rather than merely watching them. Artificial intelligence may create personalised content tailored precisely to individual tastes. The line between passive viewing and active participation will likely blur. While thrilling, I hope this does not come at the expense of the shared, communal joys that entertainment can foster and preserve.

Vocabulary: communal · foster · preserve

Q6Problem & Solution
How can people avoid spending too much time on online entertainment?

Self-discipline and awareness are key. Setting clear time limits and using apps that track usage can prevent hours from slipping away unnoticed. Consciously scheduling other activities, like exercise, hobbies or socialising, ensures entertainment does not crowd out everything else. It also helps to remove the constant temptation, perhaps by keeping devices out of reach. Essentially, treating entertainment as a reward rather than a default keeps it in balance.

Vocabulary: crowd out · reward · balance

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of on-demand entertainment?

The advantages are obvious: unprecedented convenience, choice and control over what and when we watch. It is tailored to our tastes and available instantly. The disadvantages, however, include the temptation to binge excessively, which can waste time and disrupt sleep. The sheer abundance of choice can also be paralysing, and it can isolate us. So it offers wonderful freedom, but demands self-control to enjoy without overindulging.

Vocabulary: paralysing · self-control · overindulging

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say people are becoming too passive in how they spend their free time. Do you agree?

I have some sympathy with this view. There is a real tendency to default to passive consumption, like endless scrolling or binge-watching, which requires little effort and can be mindless. This may come at the expense of more active, enriching pursuits like exercise, hobbies or socialising. However, I would not generalise too broadly, as many people remain active. It is more a temptation to guard against than an inevitable decline.

Vocabulary: mindless · enriching · guard against

9. Technology & Children

Screen time, online safety, and technology's role in childhood.

Q1Opinion
At what age do you think children should be allowed to use technology?

This is a difficult question with no perfect answer. I believe very young children should have extremely limited screen time, as their development depends on real-world play and interaction. As they grow, technology can be gradually introduced, ideally in an educational and supervised way. So rather than a strict age, I favour a gradual, age-appropriate approach, always emphasising quality of content over quantity of time.

Vocabulary: gradual · age-appropriate · quality

Q2Comparison
How is childhood today different because of technology?

Technology has fundamentally reshaped childhood. Today's children are digital natives, growing up with screens and instant access to information their parents never had. Much of their play, learning and socialising now happens online. While this offers incredible opportunities, it also means less unstructured outdoor play and more sedentary time. So childhood is arguably more stimulating but also more screen-bound than it was a generation ago.

Vocabulary: sedentary · stimulating · screen-bound

Q3Cause & Effect
What effects can too much screen time have on children?

The potential effects are concerning. Excessive screen time is linked to problems with sleep, attention and physical health due to inactivity. It may also hinder the development of social skills and language if it replaces real interaction. There are concerns about exposure to unsuitable content too. So while technology has benefits, an excess can genuinely impair a child's healthy development, both physically and socially.

Vocabulary: hinder · excess · impair

Q4Social Impact
How can technology be used positively in children's education?

Used well, technology can be a wonderful educational tool. Interactive apps and games can make learning engaging and cater to different learning styles. It gives children access to a vast world of knowledge and can help those with special needs. Educational videos can explain complex ideas vividly. The key is that it should be used purposefully as a supplement to good teaching, not as a substitute for it.

Vocabulary: purposefully · supplement · substitute

Q5Future
Do you think future generations will be even more dependent on technology?

Almost certainly, yes. As technology becomes ever more woven into every aspect of life, future generations will grow up with it as an inseparable part of their existence. This dependence will likely deepen, which brings both opportunities and risks. My hope is that alongside this, we will develop a stronger culture of digital wellbeing, teaching children to use technology intentionally and to maintain a healthy balance rather than being consumed by it.

Vocabulary: intentionally · balance · consumed

Q6Problem & Solution
How can parents keep their children safe online?

Parents can take several practical steps. Using parental controls and age-appropriate settings helps filter unsuitable content. Keeping devices in shared family spaces rather than bedrooms allows for supervision. Most importantly, maintaining open, honest communication so children feel able to report anything troubling is vital. Educating children about online risks, like strangers and scams, empowers them. Ultimately, a combination of supervision and education is the best protection.

Vocabulary: empowers · supervision · education

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of children learning to code at a young age?

The advantages are significant. Coding teaches logical thinking, problem-solving and creativity, and it equips children with a highly valuable skill for the future job market. It can also be genuinely fun and empowering. The potential disadvantages are that it adds to screen time and academic pressure, and it should not crowd out other essential activities like play and physical exercise. So it is a valuable skill, provided it is kept in balance and not forced.

Vocabulary: crowd out · balance · forced

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say technology is robbing children of a real childhood. Do you agree?

I partly agree, though I think it is a little overstated. There is a legitimate concern that excessive screen time is replacing the outdoor play, imagination and face-to-face friendships that define a healthy childhood. However, technology also offers genuine benefits and is simply the reality of the modern world. So rather than demonising it, I believe the answer lies in moderation, ensuring children still enjoy a rich, balanced upbringing.

Vocabulary: demonising · moderation · balanced
