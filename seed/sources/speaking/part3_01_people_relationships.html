1. Role Models

Famous or local figures who inspire others through their actions.

Q1Opinion
Do you think it is important for young people to have role models?

I believe it is absolutely vital for adolescents to have someone to look up to. Role models act as a kind of moral compass, offering guidance on how to navigate life. When a young person admires a professional, they are internalising the values of hard work and perseverance that led to that success. Without such figures, young people can feel a little unmoored, lacking any real sense of direction.

Vocabulary: internalising · perseverance · unmoored

Q2Comparison
How have the types of role models changed since your grandparents' time?

There has been a rather dramatic shift, I would say. In my grandparents' era, role models tended to be figures within the immediate community, such as teachers or elders. Today, influence is increasingly dominated by celebrities and social media personalities, many of whom are famous for very little of substance. I find this shift somewhat concerning, because fame has become almost entirely disconnected from genuine merit.

Vocabulary: dominated · disconnected · merit

Q3Social Impact
How do role models affect the way people behave in a society?

They have a profound ripple effect, in my opinion. When influential figures behave ethically, they set a positive standard that others tend to emulate. Conversely, when role models behave badly, it can normalise that behaviour, particularly among impressionable young people. So I would argue they play a significant part in shaping a society's collective values.

Vocabulary: emulate · normalise · collective

Q4Cause & Effect
Why do some public figures fail to remain positive role models over time?

I think the main reason is the immense pressure and scrutiny that come with public life. Constant attention can distort a person's judgement, and some become complacent once they achieve fame. There is also the temptation of wealth and power, which can gradually erode the very values that made them admirable in the first place. Sadly, staying grounded under that spotlight is genuinely difficult.

Vocabulary: scrutiny · complacent · erode

Q5Future
In the future, do you think AI or fictional characters could become role models?

It is quite possible, and to some extent it is already happening. Virtual influencers are becoming increasingly prevalent, and young people do form attachments to fictional heroes. However, I would be somewhat wary of this trend, because a fabricated character lacks the real struggles that make human role models relatable. I think something genuinely authentic would be lost in that shift.

Vocabulary: prevalent · wary · authentic

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to encourage young people to choose positive role models?

Education is key, I believe. Parents and schools should actively help children think critically about who they admire and why. Rather than simply banning certain influences, which rarely works, we should cultivate media literacy so young people can distinguish substance from mere celebrity. Highlighting inspiring local figures would also provide more attainable and grounded examples to follow.

Vocabulary: critically · cultivate · attainable

Q7Comparison
Do you think people are more influenced by role models in real life or in the media?

That is an interesting question. I would argue that media figures have a wider reach, but real-life role models, like a parent or mentor, have a far deeper and more lasting impact. The influence of a media personality is often superficial and fleeting, whereas someone you interact with daily shapes your character in a much more meaningful way.

Vocabulary: lasting · superficial · meaningful

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some people say governments should promote positive role models. Do you agree?

I partly agree. Governments can certainly showcase admirable citizens, such as scientists or humanitarians, to counterbalance shallow celebrity culture. However, I am slightly cautious, because state-endorsed role models can easily become propaganda if handled poorly. So while the intention is commendable, it needs to be done transparently and without any political agenda.

Vocabulary: showcase · propaganda · transparently

2. Family & Relationships

Changing family dynamics and the importance of relationships in modern life.

Q1Opinion
Do you think family is as important today as it was in the past?

I would say family remains just as important, though its form has changed. The emotional support and sense of belonging a family provides are timeless human needs. That said, modern life has certainly strained family bonds, with people moving away for work and spending less time together. So while its importance is undiminished, sustaining it now requires more conscious effort.

Vocabulary: belonging · strained · undiminished

Q2Comparison
How are family relationships different now compared to a generation ago?

The biggest difference is probably the shift from large, extended families to smaller, more nuclear ones. In the past, several generations often lived under one roof, which fostered close bonds. Today, families are more dispersed, and technology has, ironically, both connected and distanced us. Relationships are more equal now, but arguably less tightly knit.

Vocabulary: nuclear · dispersed · distanced

Q3Cause & Effect
Why do you think many families spend less time together nowadays?

Several factors are at play. The demands of modern careers are relentless, leaving parents exhausted and time-poor. On top of that, digital devices have become a real distraction, with family members often absorbed in their own screens even when in the same room. This gradual erosion of shared time is genuinely detrimental to close relationships.

Vocabulary: relentless · distraction · detrimental

Q4Social Impact
What role does the extended family play in your culture?

In my culture, the extended family remains absolutely central. Grandparents, uncles and cousins are deeply involved in one another's lives, providing a strong support network. This interdependence means that responsibilities, from childcare to financial help, are often shared. I think this collective approach offers a real sense of security, though it can occasionally feel a little intrusive.

Vocabulary: interdependence · collective · intrusive

Q5Future
How do you think family structures will change in the future?

I anticipate that families will continue to become smaller and more diverse in form. We will likely see more single-parent households and blended families becoming commonplace and widely accepted. Technology may also allow far-flung relatives to stay connected more easily. Overall, I think the definition of family will grow more flexible and inclusive.

Vocabulary: diverse · commonplace · flexible

Q6Problem & Solution
What can families do to stay close despite busy modern lives?

I think the key is being intentional about spending time together. Simple rituals, like sharing at least one meal a day without phones, can make a real difference. Families could also prioritise occasional trips or activities that create shared memories. Ultimately, closeness does not happen by accident; it has to be actively nurtured amid the busyness.

Vocabulary: intentional · prioritise · nurtured

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of living with extended family?

The main advantage is the constant support; there is always someone to help with childcare or during a crisis, which is invaluable. It also passes down traditions and keeps bonds strong. On the downside, it can mean a lack of privacy and occasional friction between generations with differing views. So it is really a trade-off between solidarity and personal independence.

Vocabulary: invaluable · friction · solidarity

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some believe children should care for their parents in old age. Do you agree?

I strongly agree, at least in principle. In my culture, caring for elderly parents is seen as a moral obligation, a way of repaying the sacrifices they made. That said, I recognise this is not always practical in modern life, so it need not mean living together. What matters is that the elderly are not neglected and retain their dignity.

Vocabulary: obligation · neglected · dignity

3. Friendship

The evolving role of friends and social networks today.

Q1Opinion
Do you think it is better to have a few close friends or many acquaintances?

I firmly believe that quality trumps quantity when it comes to friendship. A few close friends who genuinely understand you provide far deeper support than a large circle of superficial acquaintances. While it is useful to have a wide network for professional reasons, the emotional intimacy of a true friendship is something that simply cannot be replicated at scale.

Vocabulary: superficial · intimacy · replicated

Q2Comparison
How has technology changed the way people make friends?

Technology has transformed it enormously. People now form friendships online with others across the globe, which was unimaginable a generation ago. This has made connection more accessible, but I would argue these digital friendships can be more fragile. Meeting through a screen sometimes lacks the depth that comes from sharing real experiences face-to-face.

Vocabulary: accessible · fragile · face-to-face

Q3Cause & Effect
Why do some childhood friendships fade over time?

I think it is largely a natural consequence of people growing and changing. As we mature, our interests, values and circumstances diverge, and friends who once had everything in common may drift apart. Physical distance, when people move for work or study, only accelerates this. It is sad, but I see it as an almost inevitable part of life.

Vocabulary: diverge · accelerates · inevitable

Q4Social Impact
How important are friends compared to family in a person's life?

Both are essential, though they serve different roles. Family offers unconditional support and a sense of roots, whereas friends are people we actively choose, which makes those bonds uniquely affirming. Friends often understand aspects of our lives that family cannot. I would say a fulfilling life requires a healthy balance of both, as each provides a distinct kind of companionship.

Vocabulary: affirming · balance · companionship

Q5Future
Do you think online friendships will replace face-to-face ones in the future?

I doubt they will replace them entirely, but they will certainly become more significant. Online friendships are convenient and can be genuinely meaningful. However, humans are inherently social creatures who crave physical presence, so I believe in-person connection is irreplaceable. The most likely outcome is a hybrid, where the two forms coexist and complement each other.

Vocabulary: significant · irreplaceable · hybrid

Q6Problem & Solution
What can people do to maintain long-distance friendships?

Consistent effort is really the crucial ingredient. Regular video calls and messages can keep a friendship alive despite the distance. I think it also helps to be deliberate about planning occasional visits, as nothing quite replaces meeting in person. Ultimately, both parties have to be committed, because a friendship that only one person sustains rarely lasts.

Vocabulary: deliberate · committed · sustains

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the benefits and drawbacks of having friends from different cultures?

The benefits are considerable. Friends from other cultures broaden your perspective and challenge your assumptions, which is deeply enriching. You learn to be more tolerant and open-minded. The only real drawback might be occasional misunderstandings arising from different customs or communication styles. But in my view, these minor obstacles are easily outweighed by the rewards.

Vocabulary: enriching · tolerant · obstacles

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say social media has made friendships shallower. Do you agree?

I largely agree with that sentiment. Social media encourages us to accumulate hundreds of contacts while investing little in any of them. The constant highlight reels can also breed comparison rather than genuine connection. That said, it depends how you use it; for maintaining existing bonds it is useful. Used as a substitute for real intimacy, though, it does trivialise friendship.

Vocabulary: accumulate · trivialise · substitute

4. Parenting & Childhood

Parenting strategies, discipline, and the changing nature of childhood today.

Q1Opinion
What do you think are the most important qualities of a good parent?

In my view, the most important qualities are patience and consistency. Children thrive on stability, so a parent who sets clear, fair boundaries helps them feel secure. Equally vital is unconditional love balanced with the willingness to discipline when necessary. A good parent also leads by example, since children tend to absorb behaviour far more than instructions.

Vocabulary: consistency · discipline · absorb

Q2Comparison
How is childhood today different from childhood in the past?

Childhood has changed dramatically. In the past, children spent most of their free time playing outdoors and socialising in person. Today, much of that has been replaced by screens, which I find rather concerning. Children are also under far more academic pressure now. So while modern children have more opportunities, they arguably have a less carefree upbringing.

Vocabulary: concerning · pressure · carefree

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect does strict parenting have on children?

It can have mixed effects. On one hand, strict parenting can instil discipline and a strong work ethic. On the other, if it is excessively authoritarian, it may stifle a child's creativity and confidence, or even breed rebellion. In my opinion, the ideal lies in a balanced approach that is firm but also nurturing and understanding.

Vocabulary: authoritarian · stifle · nurturing

Q4Social Impact
How does a person's childhood affect them as an adult?

I think childhood is profoundly formative. The experiences and relationships we have in those early years shape our personality, our self-esteem and how we relate to others. A secure, loving childhood tends to produce confident, well-adjusted adults, whereas a troubled one can leave lasting scars. In many ways, our childhood lays the foundation for everything that follows.

Vocabulary: formative · scars · foundation

Q5Future
How do you think parenting will change in the coming decades?

I expect parenting will become increasingly shaped by technology. Parents will likely rely more on apps and online resources for guidance, and managing children's screen time will become an even bigger challenge. I also anticipate a move towards more egalitarian parenting, with responsibilities shared more equally. Hopefully there will also be greater awareness of children's emotional wellbeing.

Vocabulary: challenge · egalitarian · emotional

Q6Problem & Solution
How can parents help children develop good values?

The most effective way is by modelling those values themselves, since children imitate what they see. Beyond that, parents can use everyday situations as teaching moments, discussing honesty or kindness openly. Encouraging empathy, perhaps through community involvement, also helps. Ultimately, values are instilled gradually through consistent example rather than through occasional lectures.

Vocabulary: modelling · instilled · lectures

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the pros and cons of both parents working full time?

The clear advantage is financial; a dual income provides a higher standard of living and more opportunities for the children. It can also make both parents positive role models of independence. The downside is that it may leave less time for the children, potentially affecting the parent-child bond. It really comes down to the quality of the time spent, not just the quantity, and whether adequate childcare is in place.

Vocabulary: bond · quantity · childcare

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some people think children are given too much freedom today. Do you agree?

I partly agree. Modern parenting rightly encourages children's independence, but in some cases the pendulum has swung too far, and children lack clear boundaries. A total absence of limits can leave children feeling insecure rather than free. That said, the opposite extreme is equally harmful, so the goal should be freedom within a framework of sensible, loving guidance.

Vocabulary: insecure · guidance · framework

5. Generational Differences

Conflicts and similarities between different age groups in society.

Q1Opinion
Do you think there is a big generation gap in your country?

Yes, I think the generation gap in my country is quite pronounced, largely because of the rapid pace of change. Older generations grew up in a very different, more traditional society, while the young have been shaped by technology and global culture. This creates differing values and expectations, which can sometimes lead to tension, though it also brings a healthy diversity of perspectives.

Vocabulary: pronounced · tension · diversity

Q2Comparison
What are the main differences between the older and younger generations?

The differences are quite stark. The older generation generally values stability, tradition and hard-earned security, whereas young people tend to prioritise flexibility, self-expression and work-life balance. There is also a huge gap in digital fluency. However, I think these differences are often exaggerated; at their core, both generations share many of the same fundamental aspirations.

Vocabulary: digital · exaggerated · aspirations

Q3Cause & Effect
Why do older and younger generations sometimes struggle to understand each other?

The root cause is that they have lived through fundamentally different eras. Their formative experiences, technologies and social norms were worlds apart, which shapes how they see everything. This can lead to a failure to empathise, with each side viewing the other's values as alien. A lack of open, patient communication only widens that gulf.

Vocabulary: empathise · alien · widens

Q4Social Impact
How can different generations learn from one another?

There is enormous potential here. Younger people can help older generations navigate technology, while the elderly offer invaluable wisdom and life experience that no textbook can provide. This kind of exchange, sometimes called reverse mentoring, is mutually beneficial. When generations genuinely listen to each other, both are enriched, and society as a whole becomes more cohesive.

Vocabulary: invaluable · beneficial · enriched

Q5Future
Do you think the generation gap will grow larger in the future?

It is a real possibility, given how quickly technology now evolves. Each new generation is being shaped by tools the previous one barely understands, which could amplify the divide. On the other hand, constant connectivity might actually help bridge it. So the outcome is uncertain, and it may depend on whether we make a deliberate effort to stay connected across ages.

Vocabulary: amplify · connected · evolves

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to reduce conflict between generations?

Fostering genuine dialogue is essential. Creating opportunities for generations to interact meaningfully, whether in families or communities, builds mutual understanding. Both sides need to approach differences with humility rather than judgement. I also think education plays a role in teaching young people to respect their elders while encouraging older people to remain receptive to new ideas.

Vocabulary: humility · respect · receptive

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of a workplace with mixed age groups?

The advantages are significant. A mix of ages brings together the fresh ideas and tech-savviness of the young with the experience and prudence of older staff, which is a powerful combination. The potential disadvantage is friction arising from clashing work styles or communication preferences. But with good management, this diversity of thought is far more of an asset than a liability.

Vocabulary: prudence · diversity · liability

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say younger generations have life easier than older ones. Do you agree?

I think it is more nuanced than that. In material terms, young people today certainly enjoy comforts and technology their grandparents lacked. However, they face their own distinct pressures, such as intense competition, job insecurity and a constant online spotlight. So I would not say life is simply easier; rather, the hardships have shifted in nature.

Vocabulary: pressures · hardships · nature

6. Leadership

The qualities and responsibilities of effective leaders in various fields.

Q1Opinion
What qualities make someone a good leader?

In my opinion, a good leader combines vision with genuine integrity. They need the ability to inspire and motivate others towards a common goal, but also the humility to listen. Crucially, effective leaders lead by example and take responsibility for failures rather than shifting blame. I think this accountability, paired with genuine empathy, is what earns lasting respect.

Vocabulary: integrity · accountability · empathy

Q2Comparison
Is it better for a leader to be respected or feared?

I firmly believe respect is far more effective than fear. A leader who rules through fear may achieve short-term compliance, but this breeds resentment and stifles honest communication. A respected leader, by contrast, inspires genuine loyalty and willing effort. People follow such a leader out of conviction rather than intimidation, which produces far better and more sustainable results.

Vocabulary: compliance · conviction · sustainable

Q3Cause & Effect
What happens to an organisation when it has poor leadership?

The consequences can be severe. Poor leadership often results in low morale, as employees feel directionless and undervalued. This typically leads to reduced productivity and high staff turnover. Without a clear vision, an organisation can quickly stagnate or lose its way entirely. In short, weak leadership tends to permeate every level and undermine the whole enterprise.

Vocabulary: directionless · stagnate · permeate

Q4Social Impact
How important is leadership in bringing about social change?

Leadership is often the decisive factor. Throughout history, major social movements have been galvanised by charismatic leaders who could articulate a shared vision and mobilise people. A strong leader gives a movement focus and momentum. That said, I think the best leaders empower others rather than dominate, ensuring the change is collective rather than dependent on one individual.

Vocabulary: articulate · momentum · collective

Q5Future
How do you think leadership styles will change in the future?

I believe we are moving away from the traditional top-down, authoritarian model towards something more collaborative. Future leaders will likely need to be more emotionally intelligent and adaptable, given how quickly the world changes. There will also be greater emphasis on transparency and inclusivity. In short, leadership is becoming less about command and more about facilitation.

Vocabulary: collaborative · transparency · facilitation

Q6Problem & Solution
How can society develop better leaders?

I think it starts with education that nurtures not just intelligence but character and empathy. Providing young people with genuine opportunities to take responsibility, through mentorship or community projects, helps cultivate leadership skills early. We should also reward ethical leadership rather than mere ambition. Essentially, good leaders are developed over time, not simply born.

Vocabulary: cultivate · reward · developed

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of a young person becoming a leader?

The advantages include fresh energy, innovative ideas and a willingness to challenge outdated conventions. Young leaders can be genuinely dynamic. The main disadvantage is a lack of experience, which may lead to impulsive decisions or difficulty commanding respect from older colleagues. Ideally, a young leader would be paired with experienced mentors to balance their enthusiasm with wisdom.

Vocabulary: dynamic · impulsive · mentors

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say great leaders are born, not made. Do you agree?

I largely disagree with that idea. While some people may have a natural charisma or confidence, I firmly believe leadership is a set of skills that can be learned and refined through experience. History is full of leaders who grew into the role. To claim leaders are simply born undermines the value of hard work and personal development.

Vocabulary: charisma · undermines · development

7. Fame & Celebrity

The impact of celebrity culture and the pursuit of fame on society.

Q1Opinion
Why do you think so many young people want to be famous?

I think it is largely driven by social media, which has made fame appear both glamorous and easily attainable. Young people see influencers achieving wealth and adoration seemingly overnight, which is highly seductive. There is also a deeper human desire for validation and significance. Unfortunately, this often means chasing fame as an end in itself rather than as a by-product of genuine achievement.

Vocabulary: glamorous · seductive · achievement

Q2Comparison
How is fame today different from fame in the past?

The nature of fame has changed completely. In the past, fame was usually earned through exceptional talent or accomplishment, and famous people were relatively few. Today, anyone can become famous overnight through a viral video, and fame is often fleeting and detached from any real skill. Modern celebrity is also far more invasive, with stars under constant surveillance.

Vocabulary: fleeting · invasive · surveillance

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect does celebrity culture have on young people?

The effects are often detrimental, in my view. Constant exposure to celebrities' curated, idealised lives can create unrealistic expectations and fuel insecurity, especially about appearance. It can also promote materialism and a distorted sense of what matters. That said, positive celebrities who champion good causes can be a genuinely inspiring influence, so it is not entirely negative.

Vocabulary: detrimental · fuel · inspiring

Q4Social Impact
Do celebrities have a responsibility to be good role models?

I would argue they do, whether they like it or not. Given the enormous influence they wield over millions of impressionable fans, celebrities have a certain moral obligation to behave responsibly. Their actions can genuinely shape public attitudes. However, it is also unfair to expect them to be flawless, since they are human. The key is a basic level of accountability and awareness.

Vocabulary: obligation · accountability · awareness

Q5Future
Do you think celebrity culture will become more or less influential in the future?

I suspect it will become even more influential, but also more fragmented. Rather than a few global superstars, we are likely to see countless niche influencers, each commanding devoted audiences. Technology like virtual influencers may further blur the line between real and fabricated fame. Whether this influence is healthy will depend on whether audiences become more discerning and sceptical.

Vocabulary: fragmented · discerning · sceptical

Q6Problem & Solution
How can society reduce the negative effects of celebrity obsession?

Media literacy education is crucial here. Teaching young people to critically evaluate the polished images they see online can lessen the harmful comparisons. Promoting a broader range of admirable figures, such as scientists or activists, would also help rebalance what we celebrate. Ultimately, encouraging people to derive self-worth from their own lives rather than idolising others is the real solution.

Vocabulary: evaluate · rebalance · idolising

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of being famous?

The obvious advantages are wealth, influence and opportunities that most people can only dream of. Fame also provides a platform to promote worthy causes. However, the disadvantages are considerable: a total loss of privacy, relentless scrutiny, and immense pressure that can take a serious toll on mental health. Many celebrities find the constant spotlight genuinely suffocating, so it is far from the idyllic life it appears.

Vocabulary: scrutiny · suffocating · idyllic

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some people say celebrities are paid too much. Do you agree?

I have some sympathy with that view. It does seem disproportionate that an entertainer can earn more in a day than a nurse earns in a year, when the latter arguably contributes more to society. On the other hand, their pay simply reflects the market and the vast revenue they generate. So while it feels morally questionable, it is economically logical.

Vocabulary: disproportionate · questionable · logical

8. Trust & Honesty

The importance of integrity and trust in personal and public life.

Q1Opinion
Why is honesty important in relationships?

Honesty is really the bedrock of any healthy relationship. Without it, trust cannot exist, and trust is what allows people to feel safe and secure with one another. Even small lies, once discovered, can erode that foundation and breed suspicion. In my view, being truthful, even when it is difficult, is what gives a relationship genuine depth and resilience.

Vocabulary: bedrock · erode · depth

Q2Comparison
Do you think people are more or less honest than they were in the past?

That is a difficult question. I do not think human nature has fundamentally changed, but the opportunities for deception have multiplied with technology, from online scams to fake identities. At the same time, the internet has made it much harder to hide dishonesty, since misdeeds are quickly exposed. So perhaps we are no less honest, just more transparent.

Vocabulary: deception · exposed · transparent

Q3Cause & Effect
What happens to a society when trust in institutions breaks down?

The consequences are deeply damaging. When people lose faith in institutions like government or the media, it breeds cynicism and division. Cooperation becomes difficult, conspiracy theories can flourish, and social cohesion begins to unravel. Trust is essentially the glue that holds a society together, so its breakdown can lead to genuine instability and dysfunction.

Vocabulary: cynicism · flourish · instability

Q4Social Impact
Is it ever acceptable to tell a lie?

I think there are rare cases where a so-called white lie is justifiable, particularly when the truth would cause needless harm and the lie is genuinely benevolent. Sparing someone's feelings is a common example. However, this is a slippery slope, because habitual lying, even with good intentions, can undermine trust. So while not absolute, honesty should be the strong default.

Vocabulary: benevolent · undermine · default

Q5Future
Do you think it will be harder to trust information in the future?

Unfortunately, I think it will. With the rise of sophisticated technology like deepfakes and AI-generated content, distinguishing genuine information from fabricated material will become increasingly difficult. This could make people either dangerously gullible or excessively cynical. Developing strong critical-thinking skills and reliable verification tools will be absolutely essential to navigate this new landscape.

Vocabulary: fabricated · gullible · navigate

Q6Problem & Solution
How can trust be rebuilt once it has been broken?

Rebuilding trust is a slow and difficult process. It requires the person who broke it to take full responsibility and offer a sincere apology, without excuses. More importantly, they must demonstrate changed behaviour consistently over time, since words alone are not enough. The wronged party, meanwhile, needs to be willing to gradually reconcile. Essentially, trust is rebuilt through actions, not promises.

Vocabulary: responsibility · consistently · reconcile

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the benefits and risks of being completely honest all the time?

The great benefit of total honesty is that it builds a reputation for integrity, and people know exactly where they stand with you. It fosters deep trust. The risk, however, is that unfiltered honesty can sometimes be hurtful or tactless if not delivered with care. So I think the ideal is honesty tempered with diplomacy, being truthful but also kind.

Vocabulary: integrity · tactless · diplomacy

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say honesty is always the best policy. Do you agree?

I largely agree, though I would add a small caveat. As a general principle, honesty builds trust and spares us the burden of maintaining lies, so it is almost always wise. However, I think it must be balanced with compassion; brutal honesty that needlessly wounds someone is not virtuous. So honesty is the best policy, provided it is delivered sensitively.

Vocabulary: caveat · compassion · sensitively

9. Peer Pressure

Peer influence, social conformity, and parental guidance.

Q1Opinion
Do you think peer pressure is always a bad thing?

Not at all, and this is a common misconception. While peer pressure is usually associated with negative behaviour, it can be a powerful positive force too. If your peers are ambitious and disciplined, their influence can motivate you to raise your own standards. So peer pressure is really a double-edged sword; its effect depends entirely on the company you keep and how discerning you are.

Vocabulary: motivate · company · discerning

Q2Comparison
Who has more influence on teenagers, their friends or their parents?

During the teenage years specifically, I would argue friends often have the greater immediate influence. Adolescents are forming their own identity and naturally seek acceptance from their peers, sometimes at the expense of parental advice. However, parents provide the deeper, underlying values that tend to resurface later in life. So peers shape day-to-day behaviour, while parents shape long-term character.

Vocabulary: underlying · resurface · character

Q3Cause & Effect
Why are young people especially vulnerable to peer pressure?

It largely comes down to their developmental stage. Adolescents have a deep psychological need to belong and fear social rejection intensely. Their sense of identity is still forming, which makes them more susceptible to conforming with the group. Additionally, the part of the brain governing impulse control is not yet fully developed, so they are more likely to act on that pressure.

Vocabulary: belong · susceptible · pressure

Q4Social Impact
How does the desire to fit in affect people's behaviour?

The desire to fit in is an extraordinarily powerful driver of behaviour. It can lead people to conform to group norms even when they privately disagree, a phenomenon that affects everything from fashion to opinions. While this social cohesion has its benefits, it can also suppress individuality and lead to poor decisions. Learning to resist this pull and think independently is a mark of real maturity.

Vocabulary: conform · cohesion · independently

Q5Future
Do you think social media has made peer pressure stronger?

Absolutely, I think it has intensified it dramatically. In the past, peer pressure was confined to your immediate social circle, but now it is constant and global. People compare themselves to countless others online, and the pressure to project a perfect image is relentless. This amplified, round-the-clock pressure is, I believe, taking a serious toll on young people's wellbeing.

Vocabulary: relentless · amplified · wellbeing

Q6Problem & Solution
How can young people be helped to resist negative peer pressure?

Building self-confidence is the most effective safeguard. A young person with a strong sense of self is far less likely to succumb to pressure. Parents and teachers can help by fostering open communication, so children feel able to discuss difficult situations. Teaching practical strategies, like how to say no gracefully, also empowers them to stand firm.

Vocabulary: succumb · strategies · empowers

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the positive and negative sides of wanting to be like others?

On the positive side, wanting to emulate others can inspire self-improvement; admiring successful people can push us to work harder. It also helps social integration. The negative side is that it can lead to a loss of individuality and constant, unhealthy comparison. It may cause people to suppress their true selves to fit in. The healthiest approach is to draw inspiration from others while staying true to who you are.

Vocabulary: integration · suppress · inspiration

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say peer pressure decreases as people get older. Do you agree?

I broadly agree, though it never disappears entirely. As people mature, they generally develop a firmer sense of identity and care less about others' approval, making them more resilient to pressure. However, the pressure simply takes on a more subtle form in adulthood, such as keeping up with colleagues' lifestyles. So it lessens, but it certainly does not vanish.

Vocabulary: resilient · subtle · vanish

10. Conflict & Mediation

Interpersonal disputes, emotional intelligence, and resolving disagreements.

Q1Opinion
Do you think conflict between people is always negative?

Not necessarily. While conflict is uncomfortable, it can actually be constructive if handled well. Disagreements often bring underlying problems to the surface, which allows them to be addressed rather than festering. Healthy conflict can also spark better ideas and deeper understanding. The key is whether it is managed respectfully; it is unresolved or hostile conflict that is truly damaging.

Vocabulary: constructive · respectfully · damaging

Q2Comparison
Is it better to confront a problem directly or avoid it?

In most cases, I believe confronting a problem directly is far healthier. Avoidance may keep the peace temporarily, but the underlying issue tends to fester and grow worse over time. Addressing it calmly and early usually leads to genuine resolution. That said, direct confrontation should be done tactfully, choosing the right moment, so it does not escalate into a bigger dispute.

Vocabulary: fester · tactfully · escalate

Q3Cause & Effect
What are the main causes of conflict between people?

In my experience, most conflict stems from poor communication and misunderstanding rather than genuine malice. When people fail to express themselves clearly or make assumptions about others' intentions, friction inevitably arises. Differences in values, competition over resources and simple miscommunication are also common triggers. Interestingly, many disputes could be avoided entirely with a little more patience and clarity.

Vocabulary: malice · miscommunication · clarity

Q4Social Impact
How important is emotional intelligence in resolving conflicts?

It is absolutely crucial. Emotional intelligence allows a person to understand not only their own feelings but also the perspective of the other party, which is the foundation of any resolution. Someone with high emotional intelligence can stay calm, empathise, and communicate without inflaming the situation. Without this ability, people tend to react defensively, which almost always makes the conflict worse.

Vocabulary: empathise · defensively · worse

Q5Future
Do you think people will become better at resolving conflicts in the future?

I am cautiously optimistic. There is growing awareness of the importance of emotional intelligence and communication skills, which are increasingly taught in schools and workplaces. If this trend continues, future generations may be better equipped to handle disagreements. On the other hand, online communication can sometimes make people more polarised and less willing to compromise, so it is not guaranteed.

Vocabulary: equipped · polarised · compromise

Q6Problem & Solution
What is the best way to resolve a serious disagreement?

The most effective approach usually involves calm, honest dialogue where both sides genuinely listen to one another. It helps to focus on the specific issue rather than attacking the person, and to seek a compromise where possible. In some cases, involving a neutral third party as a mediator can be invaluable. Ultimately, a willingness to see the other's perspective is what unlocks resolution.

Vocabulary: compromise · mediator · perspective

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of using a mediator in disputes?

The main advantage is that a neutral mediator can defuse tension and help both parties communicate objectively, often reaching a solution they could not manage alone. It is also usually cheaper and quicker than legal action. The disadvantage is that mediation relies on both parties' goodwill; if one side is uncooperative, it can fail. It also lacks the binding authority of a court ruling.

Vocabulary: objectively · uncooperative · authority

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say avoiding conflict keeps relationships healthier. Do you agree?

I largely disagree with that. While constant conflict is obviously harmful, consistently avoiding it is not healthy either, as it allows resentment to build silently. Relationships that never address problems often suppress genuine feelings, which eventually surface in a much larger explosion. In my view, healthy relationships are not conflict-free; they simply handle disagreement maturely and constructively.

Vocabulary: silently · suppress · maturely

11. Emotional Intelligence

Empathy, self-awareness, social regulation, soft skills, and leadership.

Q1Opinion
Do you think emotional intelligence is more important than academic intelligence?

I would argue they are both important but serve different purposes. Academic intelligence may get you a qualification, but emotional intelligence is often what determines success in life and work, since it governs how you deal with people. The ability to manage emotions, empathise and communicate is genuinely indispensable. So while I would not rank one above the other, emotional intelligence is arguably more underrated.

Vocabulary: indispensable · underrated · governs

Q2Comparison
Are emotional skills valued more in some professions than others?

Definitely. In people-facing roles such as teaching, healthcare or management, emotional intelligence is arguably the most important skill of all, as success depends on connecting with others. In more technical or solitary fields, like programming, it may seem less critical, though even there teamwork requires it. So while its importance varies, I think emotional skills are becoming increasingly valued across virtually every sector.

Vocabulary: importance · valued · sector

Q3Cause & Effect
What happens when a person lacks emotional intelligence?

A lack of emotional intelligence can cause real difficulties, both personally and professionally. Such a person may struggle to read social cues, handle criticism, or manage their own reactions, which can damage relationships. They might come across as insensitive or provoke unnecessary conflict without even realising it. Over time, this can lead to isolation and repeated interpersonal friction.

Vocabulary: insensitive · isolation · friction

Q4Social Impact
How does emotional intelligence affect a person's relationships?

It has a profound effect. People with high emotional intelligence tend to have healthier, more fulfilling relationships because they can understand and respond to others' feelings sensitively. They handle disagreements calmly and communicate their own needs clearly. This creates an atmosphere of trust and mutual understanding. In essence, emotional intelligence is the quiet skill that makes relationships genuinely harmonious.

Vocabulary: sensitively · understanding · harmonious

Q5Future
Will emotional intelligence become more important as technology advances?

I strongly believe it will. As machines and AI take over more analytical and routine tasks, the uniquely human skills, like empathy, creativity and emotional connection, will become more valuable, not less. These are precisely the abilities that technology cannot easily replicate. So paradoxically, in an increasingly automated world, emotional intelligence may become one of our most sought-after qualities.

Vocabulary: valuable · replicate · sought-after

Q6Problem & Solution
Can emotional intelligence be taught, and if so, how?

Yes, I firmly believe it can be developed, even if some people are naturally more gifted. Self-awareness can be cultivated through reflection and honest feedback. Skills like active listening and empathy can be practised deliberately. Schools could help by incorporating emotional learning into the curriculum from an early age. Like any skill, it improves with conscious effort and practice, so it is far from fixed.

Vocabulary: incorporating · practice · fixed

Q7Adv & Disadv
Are there any downsides to being highly emotionally sensitive?

There can be, yes. Being highly attuned to emotions is generally a strength, but it can occasionally become a burden. Very sensitive people may absorb others' stress and feel overwhelmed, or take criticism too personally. They can also find it hard to make tough, unpopular decisions. So the ideal is emotional sensitivity balanced with healthy boundaries and enough resilience to protect oneself.

Vocabulary: overwhelmed · boundaries · resilience

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say schools focus too much on academics and not enough on emotional skills. Do you agree?

I strongly agree with this criticism. Traditional education has long prioritised exam results above all else, often neglecting the emotional and social skills that are equally vital for a fulfilling life. Skills like managing stress, empathising and collaborating are rarely taught explicitly. I think a more holistic education, one that develops the whole person, would produce far more well-rounded and capable adults.

Vocabulary: prioritised · holistic · capable

12. Social Isolation & Loneliness

Isolation, urban alienation, psychological impacts, and community cohesion.

Q1Opinion
Why do you think loneliness is becoming more common today?

I think it is a paradox of modern life. Despite being more digitally connected than ever, many people feel increasingly isolated. Urbanisation has weakened traditional community bonds, and busy, individualistic lifestyles leave little time for genuine connection. Ironically, social media can exacerbate the problem by replacing deep relationships with superficial online interaction, leaving a real emotional void.

Vocabulary: exacerbate · superficial · void

Q2Comparison
Is loneliness worse in cities or in the countryside?

This is quite counterintuitive. You might assume the countryside, being less populated, would be lonelier, but often the opposite is true. Rural communities tend to be tight-knit, where everyone knows each other. Cities, by contrast, can be profoundly alienating; you can be surrounded by millions yet feel completely anonymous. So urban loneliness, born of that anonymity, is arguably the more acute problem.

Vocabulary: alienating · anonymous · acute

Q3Cause & Effect
What effects can long-term loneliness have on a person?

The effects can be genuinely serious. Prolonged loneliness is strongly linked to mental health problems such as depression and anxiety. It can also have physical consequences, as chronic isolation is thought to weaken the immune system and even affect longevity. Beyond that, it can trap people in a negative cycle, where low mood makes them withdraw further, deepening the isolation and their sense of despair.

Vocabulary: withdraw · isolation · despair

Q4Social Impact
Which groups of people are most at risk of social isolation?

Several groups are particularly vulnerable. The elderly are perhaps most at risk, especially those who have lost a spouse or whose family lives far away. Newcomers to a city, who lack an established network, are also susceptible, as are those with disabilities or health issues that limit their mobility. Essentially, anyone facing barriers to regular social contact is prone to becoming isolated and marginalised.

Vocabulary: vulnerable · prone · marginalised

Q5Future
Do you think loneliness will become a bigger problem in the future?

I fear it might, if current trends continue. As societies become more individualistic and people increasingly work and socialise remotely, opportunities for spontaneous human contact may diminish. An ageing population in many countries could also worsen the situation. However, I remain hopeful that growing awareness of the issue will prompt communities and governments to take preventive action.

Vocabulary: awareness · prompt · preventive

Q6Problem & Solution
What can communities do to reduce loneliness?

Communities can play a powerful role. Creating accessible public spaces and organising local events, from community gardens to social clubs, gives people natural opportunities to connect. Initiatives that specifically reach out to isolated individuals, like befriending schemes for the elderly, are especially valuable. Ultimately, fostering a culture where people look out for their neighbours can rebuild the social fabric that has frayed.

Vocabulary: isolated · fostering · fabric

Q7Adv & Disadv
Is spending time alone always a bad thing?

Certainly not; there is an important distinction between loneliness and healthy solitude. Chosen time alone can be genuinely restorative, allowing for reflection, creativity and rest. Many people find solitude essential for recharging. The problem arises only when isolation is involuntary and prolonged. So spending time alone is perfectly healthy, as long as it is balanced with meaningful connection.

Vocabulary: restorative · involuntary · balanced

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say technology is the main cause of modern loneliness. Do you agree?

I partly agree, but I think it is too simplistic to blame technology alone. It is true that excessive screen time can replace real interaction and foster unhealthy comparison. However, technology can also connect isolated people, such as those far from family. So the technology itself is neutral; the real issue is how we use it. The deeper causes lie in broader societal changes.

Vocabulary: foster · neutral · societal
