1. Traditions & Customs

The role of tradition in modern life and whether it is being lost.

Q1Opinion
Do you think traditions are still important in modern society?

I believe they are more important than ever. In a rapidly changing, globalised world, traditions provide a vital sense of identity, continuity and belonging. They connect us to our history and to one another. While some outdated customs deserve to fade, I think the core traditions that bind a community together are worth preserving. They give life a certain meaning and rhythm that modernity alone cannot provide.

Vocabulary: bind · preserving · meaning

Q2Comparison
Are young people less interested in traditions than older generations?

To some extent, yes, though it varies. Young people, being more exposed to global culture and modern lifestyles, sometimes view certain traditions as outdated or irrelevant. However, I would not say they have abandoned tradition entirely; many still take great pride in their cultural roots, just alongside a more modern outlook. So there is a shift in emphasis, but I think the connection to heritage remains, merely reinterpreted in a contemporary context.

Vocabulary: outdated · reinterpreted · context

Q3Cause & Effect
Why do you think some traditions are disappearing?

Several forces are at work. Globalisation and the dominance of Western culture have homogenised lifestyles, making some local customs seem less relevant. Urbanisation and modern, busy lives also leave less time for elaborate traditional practices. Additionally, as older generations pass away, knowledge and skills are sometimes not passed on. So it is really a combination of cultural dilution and the practical pressures of modern life eroding them.

Vocabulary: homogenised · dilution · eroding

Q4Social Impact
How do traditions help bring communities together?

Traditions play a powerful unifying role. Shared festivals, rituals and customs create a sense of collective identity and belonging, reminding people that they are part of something larger than themselves. They bring people together to celebrate or mourn collectively, strengthening social bonds. This shared heritage fosters mutual understanding and solidarity. In essence, traditions act as a kind of social glue that reinforces community cohesion.

Vocabulary: solidarity · glue · cohesion

Q5Future
Do you think traditions will survive in the future?

I am cautiously optimistic. While some traditions will inevitably fade, I believe the most meaningful ones will endure, perhaps in modified forms. There is actually a growing appreciation for heritage as a counterbalance to globalisation, with many people actively seeking to revive old customs. Technology can also help document and share them. So rather than vanishing, I think traditions will evolve, adapting to remain relevant in a changing world.

Vocabulary: endure · revive · evolve

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to preserve traditional culture?

A concerted effort is needed. Education is crucial; teaching young people about their heritage in schools fosters pride and understanding. Documenting traditions and supporting the artisans and elders who hold this knowledge is also vital. Governments and communities can organise festivals and cultural events to keep customs alive and visible. Essentially, making tradition feel relevant and engaging to the young is the key to its continuity.

Vocabulary: relevant · engaging · continuity

Q7Adv & Disadv
Are there any disadvantages to holding on to old traditions?

Yes, tradition can have a darker side. Some customs are rooted in outdated beliefs and can perpetuate inequality or harmful practices, such as those that discriminate against certain groups. Blindly following tradition can also stifle progress and individual freedom. So while I value heritage, I think it is important to be discerning, preserving what enriches us while being willing to discard customs that are genuinely harmful or unjust.

Vocabulary: perpetuate · stifle · discard

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say globalisation is destroying local cultures. Do you agree?

I partly agree, as it is a genuine threat. The spread of a dominant global culture can certainly overshadow and dilute local traditions, languages and customs, leading to a worrying uniformity. However, I would not say it is entirely destructive. Globalisation also allows cultures to share and celebrate their uniqueness on a world stage. So while the risk of homogenisation is real, cultures can also prove remarkably resilient and adaptive.

Vocabulary: overshadow · homogenisation · resilient

2. Festivals & Celebrations

The importance of festivals and how celebrations are changing.

Q1Opinion
Why do you think festivals are important?

Festivals serve several important purposes. They provide a much-needed break from routine and an occasion for joy and celebration. More deeply, they reinforce cultural identity and bring communities and families together in shared rituals. They also pass traditions from one generation to the next. In my view, this sense of collective celebration and belonging is something people fundamentally need, which is why festivals remain so cherished across cultures.

Vocabulary: collective · belonging · cherished

Q2Comparison
How are festivals celebrated differently now compared to the past?

The essence remains, but the style has changed. In the past, celebrations were often simpler and more community-focused, with everyone participating directly. Today, festivals have become more commercialised, with an emphasis on spending and consumption. Technology has also changed things; people now share their celebrations on social media. So while festivals are still meaningful, some argue they have lost a degree of authenticity, becoming more about consumerism than genuine tradition.

Vocabulary: commercialised · authenticity · consumerism

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect does commercialisation have on traditional festivals?

The effect is double-edged. On one hand, commercialisation can boost the economy and raise a festival's profile, making it more widely known. On the other, it can dilute the festival's original spiritual or cultural meaning, reducing it to a shopping opportunity. The genuine sentiment behind the celebration can get lost amid the advertising and pressure to spend. So it brings visibility, but often at the cost of authenticity and true significance.

Vocabulary: dilute · authenticity · significance

Q4Social Impact
How do festivals contribute to a country's economy?

Festivals can be a significant economic driver. They boost spending on food, gifts, clothing and travel, benefiting countless businesses. Major festivals also attract tourists, generating substantial revenue for the hospitality and transport sectors. They create seasonal employment too. So beyond their cultural value, festivals are a genuine economic stimulus, injecting money into local economies and showcasing a country's culture to a global audience, which is a real asset.

Vocabulary: stimulus · audience · asset

Q5Future
Do you think traditional festivals will remain popular in the future?

I believe they will, though they will continue to evolve. While the way we celebrate may change, the fundamental human need for community, joy and shared meaning will ensure festivals endure. There may even be a resurgence of interest as people seek authenticity in an increasingly digital world. So while some may become more modernised or commercial, I think the core appeal of festivals will persist and remain relevant for generations.

Vocabulary: persist · relevant · generations

Q6Problem & Solution
How can the true spirit of festivals be preserved?

Preserving the spirit requires conscious effort. Emphasising the values behind the festival, such as togetherness, gratitude or reflection, rather than just the material aspects, is important. Involving young people in traditional practices and explaining their meaning helps too. Communities can prioritise participation over consumption. Essentially, if we consciously focus on the essence of a festival rather than its commercial trappings, its true spirit can thrive.

Vocabulary: essence · commercial · thrive

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of large public celebrations?

Large public celebrations create a wonderful, vibrant atmosphere and a powerful sense of collective joy and unity. They boost tourism and local pride. However, the disadvantages include practical issues like overcrowding, noise, litter and strain on public services. There can also be safety concerns. So while they are culturally and economically valuable, they require careful management to minimise the disruption and ensure everyone can enjoy them safely.

Vocabulary: valuable · management · disruption

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say festivals have become too commercial. Do you agree?

I largely agree with this observation. Many festivals now seem dominated by advertising, sales and pressure to spend, which can overshadow their original cultural or spiritual meaning. The focus has shifted, in some cases, from togetherness to consumerism. That said, this is not universal, and the deeper meaning often survives beneath the commercial surface for those who seek it. So it is a real trend to resist, but not an irreversible one.

Vocabulary: overshadow · resist · irreversible

3. Food & Cuisine

Eating habits, traditional food, and the globalisation of cuisine.

Q1Opinion
How important is food to a country's culture?

Food is absolutely central to culture, in my view. It is far more than mere sustenance; it reflects a nation's history, geography, values and social customs. Sharing a meal is a universal expression of hospitality and community. Traditional dishes carry deep sentimental and cultural significance, passed down through generations. So I would say cuisine is one of the most tangible and beloved expressions of a country's identity.

Vocabulary: sentimental · tangible · identity

Q2Comparison
How have people's eating habits changed over the years?

Eating habits have changed considerably. In the past, people mostly ate home-cooked, seasonal and local food, often together as a family. Today, busy lifestyles have led to more processed food, takeaways and eating on the go, often alone. Portion sizes have grown, and international cuisine is now widely available. While this offers more variety and convenience, I think it has come at some cost to health and the communal nature of dining.

Vocabulary: health · communal · dining

Q3Cause & Effect
Why do you think fast food has become so popular?

Its popularity comes down to a few key factors. Firstly, it is convenient and quick, which suits our increasingly busy, time-poor lifestyles. Secondly, it is relatively cheap and consistently tasty, engineered to appeal to our cravings. Aggressive marketing has also made it ubiquitous and aspirational. Unfortunately, this convenience comes at a cost, as its popularity has contributed to rising rates of obesity and related health problems.

Vocabulary: ubiquitous · obesity · problems

Q4Social Impact
How does the availability of international food affect local cuisine?

It has a mixed effect. On the positive side, it enriches our culinary options and promotes cultural exchange, allowing people to enjoy dishes from around the world. This is broadening and enjoyable. However, there is a concern that the popularity of international chains could overshadow and threaten traditional local cuisine. So while it adds welcome diversity, it is important that local food traditions are also valued and preserved alongside it.

Vocabulary: overshadow · diversity · preserved

Q5Future
How do you think the way we eat will change in the future?

I anticipate significant changes driven by health and sustainability concerns. Plant-based diets and alternative proteins, like lab-grown meat, are likely to become far more mainstream as we address the environmental impact of food. Technology may also personalise nutrition. Hopefully, there will be a renewed appreciation for healthy, sustainable eating. So our diets may become more conscious and innovative, balancing convenience with health and the planet.

Vocabulary: sustainable · conscious · innovative

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to encourage people to eat more healthily?

A range of measures could help. Education about nutrition from a young age is fundamental, so people can make informed choices. Making healthy food more affordable and accessible, while perhaps taxing unhealthy options, can shift behaviour. Clear food labelling and restrictions on junk food advertising, especially to children, would also help. Ultimately, creating an environment where the healthy choice is the easy, appealing choice is the most effective approach.

Vocabulary: appealing · effective · approach

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of eating out often?

Eating out offers convenience, saves time on cooking and cleaning, and provides a pleasant social experience and variety. It can be a real treat. The disadvantages, however, are that it is expensive and often less healthy, as restaurant food tends to be higher in salt, sugar and fat, with larger portions. Doing it too often can strain both your finances and your health. So it is best enjoyed in moderation.

Vocabulary: finances · health · moderation

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say home-cooked food is always better than restaurant food. Do you agree?

I largely agree, particularly regarding health and value. Home cooking allows you to control the ingredients, ensuring meals are fresher, healthier and cheaper, and it can be a rewarding activity. There is also something uniquely comforting about a home-cooked meal. However, I would not say it is always better; skilled chefs create exquisite dishes that are hard to replicate at home. So home cooking is generally healthier, but restaurants offer expertise and occasion.

Vocabulary: comforting · exquisite · expertise

4. Travel & Tourism

Why people travel and the effects of tourism on places and people.

Q1Opinion
Why do you think people enjoy travelling?

People travel for a wonderful variety of reasons. Fundamentally, I think it satisfies a deep human curiosity and desire to escape routine. Experiencing new cultures, landscapes and cuisines is genuinely enriching and broadens one's perspective. Travel also offers relaxation, adventure and the chance to create lasting memories. For me, the greatest value is how it makes you more open-minded and appreciative of the world's incredible diversity.

Vocabulary: enriching · open-minded · diversity

Q2Comparison
How has travel changed compared to the past?

Travel has been transformed almost beyond recognition. In the past, it was expensive, slow and largely a privilege of the wealthy. Today, budget airlines and the internet have made it far more accessible and easy to organise, leading to mass tourism. We can now reach distant corners of the globe in hours. While this democratisation is positive, it has also led to overcrowding at popular sites and a more superficial, hurried style of travel.

Vocabulary: accessible · overcrowding · superficial

Q3Cause & Effect
What effects does tourism have on local communities?

Tourism has profound and mixed effects. Economically, it can bring vital income and jobs, boosting the local economy significantly. However, it can also cause problems: it may inflate local prices, strain infrastructure, and sometimes lead to the commodification of culture, where traditions are performed purely for tourists. Environmental damage is another concern. So while tourism offers real economic benefits, it must be managed sustainably to avoid harming the very communities it supports.

Vocabulary: commodification · benefits · sustainably

Q4Social Impact
How does travel affect a person's understanding of the world?

Travel is genuinely one of the best forms of education. By experiencing different cultures firsthand, people develop empathy and challenge their own assumptions and prejudices. It reveals both the vast diversity and the fundamental similarities of humanity. This tends to make people more tolerant, worldly and appreciative of what they have at home. In essence, travel broadens the mind in a way that no book or documentary can fully replicate.

Vocabulary: tolerant · broadens · replicate

Q5Future
How do you think tourism will change in the future?

I foresee several shifts. Growing environmental awareness will likely make sustainable and eco-tourism far more popular, as people seek to minimise their impact. Technology, such as virtual reality, might offer new ways to experience places remotely. There may also be a move towards more meaningful, immersive travel over mass tourism. So I expect the future of travel to be more conscious, more personalised, and hopefully more responsible.

Vocabulary: conscious · personalised · responsible

Q6Problem & Solution
How can the negative impacts of tourism be reduced?

Sustainable tourism practices are the answer. Governments can manage visitor numbers at fragile sites through limits or timed entry to prevent overcrowding. Promoting lesser-known destinations helps spread the benefits and reduce pressure on hotspots. Encouraging tourists to respect local cultures and environments, and to support local businesses, is also key. Essentially, prioritising sustainability over sheer volume can mitigate the harm while preserving the benefits.

Vocabulary: sustainability · mitigate · benefits

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of tourism for a country?

The advantages are considerable: tourism generates significant income and employment, funds infrastructure, and promotes cultural exchange and international understanding. It can be a real economic lifeline. The disadvantages include environmental degradation, overcrowding, the erosion of local culture and over-reliance on a volatile industry. So tourism is a powerful economic tool, but a country must balance the pursuit of revenue against protecting its environment and cultural integrity.

Vocabulary: lifeline · environment · integrity

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say tourism does more harm than good to destinations. Do you agree?

I think it depends entirely on how it is managed. In some over-touristed places, the harm, from environmental damage to cultural erosion and inflated living costs, is undeniable and can indeed outweigh the benefits. However, when managed responsibly and sustainably, tourism can be a tremendous force for good, funding conservation and lifting communities out of poverty. So I would not agree it is inherently harmful; the outcome hinges on responsible stewardship and planning.

Vocabulary: responsible · stewardship · planning

5. Fashion & Trends

Clothing, consumer trends, and self-expression through fashion.

Q1Opinion
Why do you think fashion is important to some people?

For many people, fashion is a genuine form of self-expression, allowing them to communicate their personality, mood and identity without words. It can boost confidence and create a sense of belonging to a particular group. For some, it is also an art form and a source of creativity. While I personally value comfort over trends, I completely understand why fashion holds such significance; it is a powerful, visual language of identity.

Vocabulary: significance · visual · identity

Q2Comparison
Do you think people care more about fashion now than in the past?

In some ways, yes. The rise of social media has made appearance more visible and, arguably, more important, with influencers constantly showcasing new trends. Fast fashion has also made trendy clothing cheap and accessible, fuelling constant consumption. However, past eras had their own strong fashion cultures too. So perhaps it is not that people care more, but that fashion has become more fast-paced, disposable and publicly displayed than ever before.

Vocabulary: fast-paced · disposable · publicly

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect does the fashion industry have on the environment?

The environmental impact is unfortunately severe, particularly from fast fashion. The industry is a major consumer of water and a significant source of pollution and carbon emissions. The culture of buying cheap, disposable clothing generates enormous textile waste, much of which ends up in landfill. So while fashion brings joy and self-expression, its current unsustainable model exacts a heavy toll on the planet that urgently needs addressing.

Vocabulary: disposable · unsustainable · toll

Q4Social Impact
How does fashion influence young people?

Fashion has a powerful hold on young people. It is closely tied to identity and social acceptance, so what they wear can strongly affect their confidence and sense of belonging. Trends spread rapidly through social media, creating pressure to keep up, which can be expensive and stressful. While fashion can be a positive outlet for creativity, this relentless pressure to conform can also breed insecurity and unhealthy comparison.

Vocabulary: stressful · relentless · comparison

Q5Future
How do you think fashion will change in the future?

I anticipate sustainability will become a driving force. As awareness of fast fashion's harm grows, I expect a shift towards more durable, ethical and recycled clothing, and a move away from throwaway culture. Technology may also personalise fashion, and digital or virtual clothing could even emerge. So I am hopeful the future of fashion will be more sustainable, more conscious, and perhaps less wasteful than the current model.

Vocabulary: sustainable · conscious · wasteful

Q6Problem & Solution
What can be done to make fashion more sustainable?

Several changes are needed. On the industry side, brands should adopt ethical, eco-friendly production and be more transparent. Consumers can play a huge role by buying less, choosing quality over quantity, and embracing second-hand or recycled clothing. Repairing and reusing garments rather than discarding them also helps. Ultimately, moving away from a throwaway mindset towards more durable, considered consumption is the most impactful solution.

Vocabulary: throwaway · durable · impactful

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of following fashion trends?

Following trends can be fun and allows people to feel current, confident and part of a community. It is a creative outlet and can help people fit in socially. The disadvantages, though, are that constantly chasing trends is expensive, contributes to environmental waste, and can suppress genuine individuality. It can also foster insecurity. So while there is joy in it, an obsessive pursuit of trends can become both costly and superficial.

Vocabulary: obsessive · costly · superficial

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say fashion is a waste of money. Do you agree?

I only partly agree. Excessive spending on constantly changing trends, particularly disposable fast fashion, can certainly be wasteful and financially imprudent. However, I would not dismiss fashion entirely, as it is a legitimate form of self-expression and creativity that brings people genuine joy and confidence. Investing in quality, lasting pieces is also sensible. So it is not inherently a waste; it becomes one only when pursued excessively and impulsively.

Vocabulary: imprudent · excessively · impulsively

6. Hobbies & Leisure

How people spend their free time and the value of leisure.

Q1Opinion
Why are hobbies important in people's lives?

Hobbies are far more valuable than people often realise. They provide a vital outlet for relaxation and stress relief, offering a welcome escape from the pressures of work. They also allow for personal growth, creativity and a sense of accomplishment outside our professional identity. Additionally, many hobbies foster social connections. In my view, hobbies are essential for a balanced, fulfilling life, nourishing parts of ourselves that work often neglects.

Vocabulary: balanced · fulfilling · neglects

Q2Comparison
How do people's leisure activities differ between generations?

There are noticeable differences. Older generations often favour traditional pastimes like gardening, reading physical books, or socialising in person. Younger people, by contrast, spend much of their leisure time on digital activities, such as gaming, streaming and social media. That said, there is considerable overlap, and interest in older hobbies is reviving among the young. So the core need for leisure is universal, but the medium has shifted towards the digital.

Vocabulary: reviving · medium · digital

Q3Cause & Effect
What are the benefits of having a creative hobby?

Creative hobbies offer numerous benefits. They provide a productive outlet for self-expression and can be deeply therapeutic, reducing stress and improving mental wellbeing. The sense of accomplishment from creating something is genuinely rewarding and boosts confidence. They also keep the mind active and can spark skills that enrich other areas of life. Essentially, creative pursuits nourish the soul and offer a fulfilment that passive entertainment simply cannot match.

Vocabulary: rewarding · enrich · match

Q4Social Impact
How does having free time affect people's quality of life?

Free time is essential for a good quality of life. It provides the space to rest, recharge and pursue the activities and relationships that bring us joy and meaning. Without adequate leisure, people risk burnout, stress and a diminished sense of wellbeing. Free time allows us to be more than just workers. So I would argue that leisure is not a luxury but a fundamental necessity for a healthy, rounded existence.

Vocabulary: necessity · healthy · rounded

Q5Future
Do you think people will have more free time in the future?

This is uncertain. Optimistically, if automation boosts productivity and society chooses to share the gains, people could work less and enjoy more leisure, as concepts like the four-day week suggest. However, there is an opposing risk that constant connectivity and economic pressure could actually erode our free time further. So the amount of leisure we have may depend less on technology and more on the choices society makes about priorities.

Vocabulary: erode · choices · priorities

Q6Problem & Solution
Many people say they have no time for hobbies. What can they do?

It often comes down to priorities rather than an absolute lack of time. People can start by consciously carving out even small pockets of time, perhaps by reducing passive activities like scrolling on their phones. Scheduling hobby time like any other commitment helps make it happen. Choosing hobbies that fit their lifestyle is also wise. Essentially, treating leisure as essential rather than optional helps people reclaim time they did not realise they had.

Vocabulary: essential · reclaim · had

Q7Adv & Disadv
Are there any downsides to spending too much time on hobbies?

There can be, if taken to an extreme. While hobbies are healthy, an obsessive devotion to one could lead to neglecting responsibilities, relationships or work. Some hobbies can also become expensive. If a hobby turns into an escape from dealing with real problems, that is unhealthy too. So like most things, hobbies are wonderful in moderation, but they should complement a balanced life rather than consume it entirely.

Vocabulary: moderation · complement · consume

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say hobbies should be useful, not just for fun. Do you agree?

I respectfully disagree with that as a rule. While hobbies that build skills are certainly valuable, I firmly believe a hobby does not need to be productive to be worthwhile. The primary purpose of leisure is enjoyment, relaxation and wellbeing, and something that brings pure joy is valuable in itself. Insisting that all free time be useful reflects an overly utilitarian mindset. Sometimes, doing something simply for pleasure is exactly the point.

Vocabulary: useful · utilitarian · pleasure

7. Consumer Behaviour & Shopping

How and why people buy, and the culture of consumption.

Q1Opinion
Do you think people buy more things than they actually need?

Yes, I think overconsumption is a defining feature of modern society. We are constantly bombarded with advertising designed to create desires for things we do not truly need. Cheap goods and easy credit make impulsive buying effortless. Many people also shop for emotional reasons, as a form of therapy. So I would say that a great deal of our consumption is driven by manufactured wants rather than genuine necessity, which is quite wasteful.

Vocabulary: manufactured · necessity · wasteful

Q2Comparison
How has shopping changed with the rise of the internet?

Online shopping has revolutionised the experience. It offers unparalleled convenience, allowing people to buy almost anything from home at any hour, with easy price comparison. This has, however, put pressure on traditional physical shops, many of which have struggled. The experience has become more efficient but arguably less social. So while the internet has made shopping far more convenient and accessible, it has also transformed our high streets, not always for the better.

Vocabulary: efficient · convenient · accessible

Q3Cause & Effect
Why do you think advertising is so influential?

Advertising is influential because it is sophisticated and pervasive. It cleverly taps into our emotions, insecurities and aspirations, associating products with happiness, status or belonging rather than mere function. Its sheer ubiquity means we are exposed to it constantly, which reinforces its messages. Modern targeted advertising is especially potent, as it is tailored precisely to our interests. So it shapes our desires far more than we consciously realise, subtly steering our behaviour.

Vocabulary: reinforces · steering · behaviour

Q4Social Impact
What impact does consumerism have on society?

Consumerism has a profound and mixed impact. It drives economic growth and provides jobs, which is beneficial. However, it also fuels materialism, encouraging people to equate happiness with possessions, which studies suggest is illusory. It contributes significantly to environmental degradation through waste and resource depletion. It can also foster debt and dissatisfaction. So while it powers the economy, unchecked consumerism carries serious social and environmental costs.

Vocabulary: illusory · environmental · costs

Q5Future
Do you think people will become more conscious consumers in the future?

I am cautiously hopeful. There is a growing awareness of the environmental and ethical impact of our purchases, and movements towards minimalism, sustainability and ethical consumption are gaining momentum. Younger generations in particular seem more values-driven in their spending. However, the powerful forces of marketing and convenience remain strong. So while I believe conscious consumption will increase, whether it becomes the norm depends on a genuine cultural shift.

Vocabulary: values-driven · increase · shift

Q6Problem & Solution
How can people be encouraged to consume more responsibly?

Education and awareness are key. Helping people understand the true environmental and social cost of their purchases can shift attitudes. Encouraging habits like buying quality over quantity, repairing rather than replacing, and choosing ethical brands makes a difference. Governments can also play a role through regulation and incentives. Ultimately, fostering a cultural value of sufficiency over excess, where we question whether we truly need something, is the most sustainable path.

Vocabulary: sufficiency · sustainable · path

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of online shopping?

The advantages are clear: convenience, a vast selection, easy price comparison and often lower prices, all from home. It is a great time-saver. The disadvantages include the inability to physically examine products before buying, delivery delays, the environmental cost of packaging and transport, and the temptation to overspend. It also lacks the social experience of physical shopping. So it offers wonderful convenience, but with some real trade-offs in experience and impact.

Vocabulary: convenience · trade-offs · experience

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say buying things makes people happy. Do you agree?

I only partly agree. There is undeniably a short-term thrill in acquiring something new, so buying can bring a fleeting sense of pleasure. However, extensive research suggests this happiness is temporary and superficial, quickly fading in a cycle sometimes called the hedonic treadmill. Lasting happiness tends to come from experiences and relationships, not possessions. So while shopping offers momentary joy, relying on it for happiness is ultimately hollow and unfulfilling.

Vocabulary: superficial · hollow · unfulfilling

8. Globalisation of Culture

How cultures influence one another in an interconnected world.

Q1Opinion
Do you think cultural globalisation is a positive development?

I see it as a genuinely double-edged phenomenon. On the positive side, it fosters greater understanding, tolerance and exchange between cultures, and lets us enjoy the best of what the world offers, from food to film. However, there is a real risk that it leads to cultural homogenisation, where dominant cultures overwhelm local ones. So while I appreciate the connection it brings, I believe we must actively work to preserve cultural diversity alongside it.

Vocabulary: homogenisation · preserve · diversity

Q2Comparison
How has globalisation changed local cultures?

The changes are significant. Local cultures have been enriched by exposure to global ideas, technologies and cuisines. However, they have also been challenged, as global brands, media and the English language spread, sometimes eclipsing local traditions and languages. The result is often a hybrid culture, blending the local and the global. So globalisation has made cultures more interconnected and diverse in some ways, yet more uniform in others.

Vocabulary: eclipsing · interconnected · uniform

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect does the spread of English have on other languages?

The spread of English as a global lingua franca has clear benefits, facilitating international communication, business and science. However, it also poses a threat to linguistic diversity. As English dominates, some smaller languages are being marginalised and, in extreme cases, are dying out, taking unique cultural knowledge with them. So while a common language is enormously useful, its dominance risks a loss of the rich linguistic heritage that makes humanity so diverse.

Vocabulary: marginalised · linguistic · heritage

Q4Social Impact
How does exposure to other cultures affect people?

Exposure to other cultures is generally very positive for people. It tends to make individuals more open-minded, tolerant and empathetic, as they realise there are many valid ways of living. It challenges stereotypes and prejudices. It can also be creatively inspiring, blending ideas in new ways. So while it can occasionally cause a sense of confusion about one's own identity, overall I believe cultural exposure broadens horizons and enriches people enormously.

Vocabulary: inspiring · broadens · enriches

Q5Future
Do you think all cultures will eventually become the same?

I do not think so, though they will certainly grow more similar in some respects. While globalisation is a powerful homogenising force, human beings have a deep-seated need for distinct identity and belonging, which tends to make cultures resilient. We are also seeing movements to actively preserve and revive local traditions. So rather than becoming identical, I expect cultures to blend and influence one another while retaining their distinctive essence.

Vocabulary: resilient · distinctive · essence

Q6Problem & Solution
How can countries protect their cultural identity in a globalised world?

There are several strategies. Governments can support and fund local arts, languages, film and traditions to ensure they thrive. Education that instils pride in one's heritage is fundamental. Protecting local media and industries from being entirely overwhelmed by global giants also helps. Crucially, this should be done not by rejecting outside influence, but by celebrating and promoting one's own culture so that it remains vibrant and valued.

Vocabulary: promoting · vibrant · valued

Q7Adv & Disadv
What are the advantages and disadvantages of a globalised world?

The advantages are substantial: greater economic opportunity, the free flow of ideas and technology, cultural exchange, and international cooperation on shared problems. It has lifted many out of poverty. The disadvantages include cultural homogenisation, growing inequality, the exploitation of cheaper labour, and increased vulnerability to global crises, as the pandemic showed. So globalisation is a powerful force for connection and progress, but one with significant inequalities and vulnerabilities.

Vocabulary: connection · inequalities · vulnerabilities

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say globalisation mainly benefits wealthy countries. Do you agree?

There is considerable truth to this, though it is not the whole picture. It is often argued that wealthy nations and large corporations reap the greatest rewards, while poorer countries may suffer exploitation and cultural erosion. The benefits are undeniably unevenly distributed. However, globalisation has also lifted hundreds of millions out of poverty in developing nations. So while the playing field is far from level, I would not say the benefits are exclusively for the privileged.

Vocabulary: unevenly · exclusively · privileged

9. Music & the Arts

The role of music and creative arts in society and individual life.

Q1Opinion
Why do you think music is important to people?

Music holds a unique and powerful place in human life. It has an extraordinary ability to evoke emotion, from joy to sorrow, and to comfort us in a way few other things can. It is also deeply tied to memory and identity, and it brings people together, whether at concerts or festivals. In my view, music is a universal language that transcends barriers and speaks directly to our emotions, which is why it is so cherished.

Vocabulary: transcends · emotions · cherished

Q2Comparison
How has the way people listen to music changed?

It has changed dramatically. In the past, people bought physical records or CDs and often listened to a whole album deliberately. Now, streaming gives us instant access to virtually all recorded music, and we tend to consume it in playlists and individual tracks. This is wonderfully convenient, but some argue it has made listening more passive and fragmented. So access has expanded enormously, but our engagement with music may have become more casual.

Vocabulary: passive · fragmented · casual

Q3Cause & Effect
What effect does learning a musical instrument have on children?

Learning an instrument has remarkably positive effects. Studies suggest it enhances cognitive development, improving skills like memory, concentration and mathematical ability. It also teaches discipline, patience and perseverance, since mastery requires consistent practice. Furthermore, it provides a creative outlet and a real sense of accomplishment that boosts confidence. So beyond the joy of making music, it offers profound developmental and character-building benefits.

Vocabulary: developmental · character-building · benefits

Q4Social Impact
How important are the arts to society?

I believe the arts are absolutely vital, though often undervalued. They enrich our lives, provoke thought, and allow us to explore and express the full range of human experience. The arts preserve culture and history, foster empathy by exposing us to different perspectives, and can even drive social change. Economically, the creative industries are significant too. So far from being a luxury, the arts are, in my view, essential to a civilised and reflective society.

Vocabulary: essential · reflective · society

Q5Future
Do you think technology will change the arts in the future?

Undoubtedly, and it already is. Technology is providing artists with powerful new tools and mediums, from digital art to AI-generated music, expanding creative possibilities. It has also democratised the arts, allowing anyone to create and share their work globally. However, this raises questions about authenticity and the role of human creativity. So while technology will transform and democratise the arts, I hope it will augment human expression rather than replace it.

Vocabulary: transform · democratise · augment

Q6Problem & Solution
Arts funding is often cut. How can the arts be better supported?

Supporting the arts requires a multi-pronged approach. Governments should recognise their cultural and economic value and provide stable funding rather than treating them as expendable. Encouraging private sponsorship and philanthropy can supplement this. Integrating arts education properly into schools ensures future generations value them. Making the arts more accessible and affordable to ordinary people also builds public support. Essentially, we must treat the arts as an investment, not a luxury.

Vocabulary: accessible · investment · luxury

Q7Adv & Disadv
Are there advantages to studying an arts subject rather than a science?

Certainly. Arts subjects develop invaluable skills like critical thinking, creativity, communication and empathy, which are highly transferable and increasingly prized by employers. They encourage us to grapple with complex human questions. The perceived disadvantage is that arts degrees are sometimes seen as less directly employable than sciences. However, I think this is often overstated, as the skills they cultivate are genuinely versatile. So both paths have distinct and equal merit.

Vocabulary: employable · versatile · merit

Q8Agree / Disagree
Some say art is not as important as science. Do you agree?

I firmly disagree with ranking them this way, as they serve different but equally vital purposes. Science advances our material understanding and improves our lives technologically, which is crucial. But art nourishes our emotional and spiritual lives, fosters empathy, and gives meaning to human existence. A world with science but no art would be efficient yet soulless. So rather than one being more important, I see them as complementary pillars of a flourishing civilisation.

Vocabulary: soulless · complementary · flourishing
