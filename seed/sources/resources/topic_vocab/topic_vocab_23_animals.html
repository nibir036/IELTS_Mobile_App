Animals & Wildlife

IELTS Academic topic vocabulary — Topic 23. Terms, meanings, and model collocations for Writing and Speaking.

TermMeaning / UsageExample collocation
wildlifewild animals living in natural conditionsThe reserve protects rare wildlife.
speciesa group of similar living organismsThe forest holds hundreds of species.
habitatthe natural home of a plant or animalLogging destroys the tiger's habitat.
ecosystema community of organisms and their environmentPollution upsets the whole ecosystem.
biodiversitythe variety of life in an areaRainforests have immense biodiversity.
faunathe animals of a regionThe island's fauna is found nowhere else.
florathe plants of a regionRich flora supports many insects.
predatoran animal that hunts others for foodThe wolf is a top predator.
preyan animal hunted by anotherRabbits are common prey for foxes.
carnivorean animal that eats meatThe lion is a powerful carnivore.
herbivorean animal that eats plantsThe deer is a gentle herbivore.
omnivorean animal that eats both plants and meatThe bear is an adaptable omnivore.
mammala warm-blooded animal that feeds its young milkThe whale is the largest mammal.
reptilea cold-blooded animal with scaly skinThe lizard is a desert reptile.
amphibianan animal able to live on land and in waterThe frog is a familiar amphibian.
species diversitythe range of different species presentWarming reduces species diversity.
endangeredat serious risk of dying outThe rhino is critically endangered.
extinctno longer existingThe bird became extinct last century.
extinctionthe dying out of a speciesHunting drove the animal to extinction.
threatenedlikely to become endangeredMany reef fish are now threatened.
vulnerableeasily harmed or at riskThe species is classed as vulnerable.
conservationthe protection of nature and wildlifeConservation saved the species from loss.
preservationkeeping something safe from harmHabitat preservation is a priority.
protectionkeeping something from damage or harmLegal protection shields rare birds.
sanctuarya safe place for wildlifeThe sanctuary shelters orphaned elephants.
reservean area set aside for wildlifeThe nature reserve bans all hunting.
national parkprotected land managed by the stateThe national park draws many visitors.
poachingthe illegal hunting of wild animalsPoaching threatens the last rhinos.
poachera person who hunts animals illegallyRangers caught the armed poacher.
huntingthe pursuit and killing of animalsUnregulated hunting wiped out the herds.
deforestationthe clearing of forestsDeforestation destroys countless habitats.
habitat lossthe destruction of animals' natural homesHabitat loss is the chief driver of decline.
encroachmentgradual intrusion onto wild landHuman encroachment shrinks the forest.
migrationthe seasonal movement of animalsThe birds' migration spans two continents.
hibernationa deep sleep through the winterBears enter hibernation in the cold months.
adaptationa change that helps an animal surviveThick fur is an adaptation to the cold.
evolutiongradual change in species over timeEvolution shaped the finches' beaks.
instincta natural, inborn pattern of behaviourSalmon return upriver by instinct.
camouflagecolouring that hides an animalThe moth's camouflage fools predators.
nocturnalactive at nightOwls are nocturnal hunters.
domesticatedtamed and kept by humansDogs were domesticated long ago.
captivitythe state of being kept, not in the wildSome animals breed poorly in captivity.
livestockfarm animals raised for useDisease spread quickly through the livestock.
breedingthe producing of young by animalsA breeding programme revived the species.
reproductionthe process of producing offspringPollution harms fish reproduction.
offspringthe young of an animalThe pair raised three offspring.
populationthe number of a species in an areaThe elephant population is falling.
declinea steady fall in number or healthInsect numbers are in sharp decline.
recoverya return to a healthy levelStrict rules aided the species' recovery.
reintroductionreleasing a species back into the wildThe wolf's reintroduction balanced the park.
rewildingrestoring land to a natural, wild stateRewilding brought beavers back to the river.
ecosystem balancethe stable relationship among speciesLosing predators upsets ecosystem balance.
food chainthe order in which organisms feed on othersToxins climb up the food chain.
food webthe network of connected food chainsRemoving one species frays the food web.
pollinatoran animal that carries pollen between plantsBees are a vital pollinator.
invasive speciesa non-native species that spreads harmfullyAn invasive species overran the lake.
native speciesa species originally found in an areaThe newcomers crowded out native species.
keystone speciesa species that holds an ecosystem togetherThe otter is a keystone species on the coast.
ecologicalrelating to the relations of organismsThe dam caused ecological damage.
ecologythe study of organisms and their environmentShe studies the ecology of wetlands.
zoologythe scientific study of animalsHis degree is in zoology.
veterinaryrelating to the care of animals' healthThe reserve has a veterinary team.
welfarethe health and comfort of animalsThe law protects animal welfare.
crueltycruel treatment of animalsCampaigners exposed the cruelty of the trade.
exploitationunfair use of animals for gainCritics condemn the exploitation of wildlife.
traffickingthe illegal trade in wild animalsWildlife trafficking is a global crime.
overfishingcatching fish faster than they can breedOverfishing emptied the once-rich banks.
overgrazingexcessive feeding that strips the landOvergrazing turned the plain to dust.
pollutionharmful substances in the environmentPlastic pollution chokes sea turtles.
climate changelong-term shifts in weather patternsClimate change is shrinking polar habitats.
ecotourismresponsible travel to natural areasEcotourism funds the reserve's work.
rangera person who guards a protected areaA ranger patrols the park at dawn.
endemicfound only in one particular areaThe lemur is endemic to the island.
thriveto grow or develop wellBirds thrive in the restored wetland.
flourishto grow strongly and healthilyWildlife began to flourish after the ban.
dwindleto shrink gradually in numberThe herd continues to dwindle.
jeopardiseto put at riskLogging jeopardises the forest's birds.
conserveto protect from loss or wasteReserves help conserve rare species.
sustainto keep something going over timeHealthy reefs sustain vast marine life.
ecological footprintthe impact of human activity on natureCutting waste lowers our ecological footprint.
symbiosisa close, mutually helpful relationshipThe bird and the buffalo live in symbiosis.
territorialdefending an area as one's ownMale lions are fiercely territorial.
scavengeran animal that feeds on dead matterThe vulture is a vital scavenger.
wildernesswild, uncultivated natural landVast wilderness still shelters large game.
genetic diversityvariety in the genes of a populationLow genetic diversity weakens a species.
repopulateto fill an area with a species againEfforts to repopulate the lake succeeded.
degradationthe decline of the natural environmentLand degradation drives animals away.
resilientable to recover from disturbanceSome reefs prove surprisingly resilient.
equilibriuma state of natural balancePredators keep the system in equilibrium.
anthropogeniccaused by human activityAnthropogenic pressure drives most declines.
