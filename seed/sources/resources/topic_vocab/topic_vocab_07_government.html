Government & Society

IELTS Academic topic vocabulary — Topic 7. Terms, meanings, and model collocations for Writing and Speaking.

TermMeaning / UsageExample collocation
governancethe way a country or organisation is run and decisions are madeTransparent governance builds public trust in institutions.
legislationlaws collectively, or the process of making themParliament passed legislation to curb carbon emissions.
policymakera person responsible for shaping official decisions and rulesPolicymakers must weigh short-term costs against long-term gains.
democracya system in which citizens choose their government by votingA functioning democracy depends on an informed electorate.
electorateall the people entitled to vote in an electionPoliticians are ultimately accountable to the electorate.
constituencya district whose voters elect a representativeShe won her constituency by a narrow margin.
referenduma direct public vote on a single political questionThe issue was settled by a national referendum.
legitimacythe quality of being accepted as rightful and lawfulA regime without popular support soon loses its legitimacy.
accountabilitythe obligation to explain and answer for one's actionsGreater accountability reduces the scope for corruption.
transparencyopenness about decisions, spending, and processesBudget transparency lets citizens track public spending.
bureaucracythe system of officials and administrative proceduresExcessive bureaucracy slows down public services.
civil servanta government employee in a non-political postExperienced civil servants ensure continuity between administrations.
public sectorthe part of the economy run by the stateThe public sector employs a large share of the workforce.
private sectorthe part of the economy owned by private businessesReforms aimed to attract private sector investment.
welfare statea system in which the government funds social supportThe welfare state protects citizens against poverty and illness.
social welfaregovernment help for people in needSpending on social welfare rose during the recession.
subsidymoney given by the state to support an activity or groupFuel subsidies place a heavy burden on the budget.
taxationthe system of imposing and collecting taxesProgressive taxation shifts the burden onto higher earners.
public spendingmoney the government spends on services and infrastructureRising public spending widened the fiscal deficit.
austeritystrict government policy to cut spending and debtAusterity measures triggered widespread protests.
fiscal policygovernment use of spending and taxation to steer the economyExpansionary fiscal policy aims to boost demand.
regulationofficial rules that control how something is doneTighter regulation curbed reckless lending.
deregulationthe removal of rules restricting an industryDeregulation of the airlines lowered ticket prices.
privatisationtransfer of a state asset to private ownershipThe privatisation of utilities remains controversial.
nationalisationtransfer of a private asset to state ownershipThe government considered nationalisation of the railways.
infrastructurebasic physical systems such as roads and powerInvestment in infrastructure underpins economic growth.
public servicesservices the state provides, like health and educationUnderfunded public services strain in times of crisis.
constitutionthe fundamental set of laws defining a stateThe reform required amending the national constitution.
sovereigntya state's full authority over itself and its territoryMembership raised concerns about loss of sovereignty.
jurisdictionthe official power to apply the law in an areaThe case fell outside the court's jurisdiction.
judiciarythe branch of government made up of the courtsAn independent judiciary safeguards the rule of law.
rule of lawthe principle that all are subject to the lawThe rule of law protects citizens from arbitrary power.
separation of powersdivision of authority among branches of governmentThe separation of powers prevents any one branch dominating.
checks and balancesmechanisms by which branches limit each otherChecks and balances restrain the executive.
executivethe branch that carries out and enforces lawsThe executive proposed a sweeping reform bill.
legislaturethe body that makes laws, such as parliamentThe legislature voted down the amendment.
parliamentthe supreme law-making assembly of a countryThe bill cleared both houses of parliament.
authoritarianfavouring strict obedience to authority over freedomThe authoritarian regime suppressed dissent.
totalitariancontrolling every aspect of public and private lifeA totalitarian state tolerates no opposition.
dictatorshiprule by a single leader with unlimited powerThe dictatorship collapsed after mass uprisings.
regimea government, especially an authoritarian oneThe regime faced growing international isolation.
corruptiondishonest use of power for private gainEntrenched corruption deters foreign investors.
briberyoffering money to influence an official improperlyAnti-bribery laws target officials and firms alike.
nepotismfavouring relatives in appointments and contractsNepotism undermines merit-based recruitment.
embezzlementtheft of funds entrusted to one's careThe official was jailed for embezzlement of public money.
reforma change intended to improve a systemStructural reform is needed to modernise the economy.
policya plan or course of action adopted by a governmentThe new policy targets youth unemployment.
initiativea new plan or programme to address a problemA government initiative encouraged recycling nationwide.
implementationthe act of putting a plan or policy into effectPoor implementation undermined an otherwise sound policy.
enforcementthe act of ensuring that laws are obeyedWeak enforcement rendered the ban ineffective.
compliancethe act of obeying rules or standardsFirms face penalties for non-compliance.
mandateauthority to act, granted by voters or a bodyThe party claimed a clear mandate for change.
incumbentthe current holder of an officeThe incumbent lost to a younger challenger.
coalitionan alliance of parties forming a government togetherA fragile coalition struggled to pass the budget.
oppositionparties not in power that challenge the governmentThe opposition accused ministers of mismanagement.
partisanstrongly supporting one party, often unfairlyPartisan gridlock stalled essential reforms.
ideologya system of political beliefs and idealsThe parties clashed over fundamental ideology.
civil societynon-governmental groups that shape public lifeA vibrant civil society holds power to account.
civil libertiesbasic individual freedoms protected from the stateEmergency laws eroded long-standing civil liberties.
human rightsrights all people hold simply by being humanThe treaty enshrines fundamental human rights.
freedom of speechthe right to express opinions without censorshipFreedom of speech is a cornerstone of democracy.
censorshipsuppression of speech or information by authorityState censorship silenced critical journalists.
surveillanceclose monitoring of people by the stateMass surveillance raises serious privacy concerns.
activismvigorous campaigning for political or social changeGrassroots activism forced the policy u-turn.
protesta public expression of objection or disapprovalPeaceful protest is a legitimate form of dissent.
dissentdisagreement with an official or majority viewThe regime treated all dissent as a threat.
advocacypublic support for a cause or policyPatient advocacy groups lobbied for better funding.
lobbyingtrying to influence lawmakers on behalf of interestsCorporate lobbying shaped the final draft of the law.
citizenshipthe status of being a legal member of a countryActive citizenship involves voting and volunteering.
franchisethe right to vote in electionsReforms extended the franchise to all adults.
representationthe act of speaking or acting for a groupWomen remain under representation in parliament.
devolutiontransfer of power from central to regional governmentDevolution gave regions control over health spending.
decentralisationspreading authority away from a central bodyDecentralisation brought services closer to citizens.
municipalitya town or district with local self-governmentThe municipality manages waste and local roads.
public opinionthe collective views of the populationPublic opinion shifted sharply against the war.
social cohesionthe sense of unity and shared belonging in a societyInequality can weaken social cohesion.
social mobilitythe ability to move between social classesQuality education raises social mobility.
inequalityunequal distribution of wealth or opportunityWidening inequality fuels social resentment.
marginalisationpushing a group to the edge of societyPolicy failed to address the marginalisation of minorities.
discriminationunfair treatment based on group membershipLaws prohibit discrimination in hiring.
equityfairness achieved by accounting for different needsThe scheme promotes equity in access to healthcare.
inclusionthe practice of involving all groups fullySocial inclusion reduces long-term welfare costs.
integrationthe process of groups becoming part of wider societyLanguage classes aid the integration of newcomers.
solidarityunity and mutual support within a groupWorkers acted in solidarity with the strikers.
philanthropyprivate giving to promote the public goodPhilanthropy funds many community projects.
volunteeringgiving unpaid time to help others or a causeVolunteering strengthens ties within a community.
communitya group living in one area or sharing an interestLocal community groups led the clean-up effort.
grassrootsordinary people at the local level, not leadersChange began as a grassroots movement.
consensusgeneral agreement among a groupThe committee reached a broad consensus on reform.
stakeholderanyone with an interest in a decision or outcomePolicy was drafted after consulting all stakeholders.
welfare provisionthe support services a state makes availableGenerous welfare provision cushions economic shocks.
social contractthe implicit agreement between citizens and the stateHigh taxes are justified by the social contract.
rights and responsibilitiesthe freedoms citizens enjoy and the duties they oweCitizenship balances rights and responsibilities.
