Media & Advertising

IELTS Academic topic vocabulary — Topic 8. Terms, meanings, and model collocations for Writing and Speaking.

TermMeaning / UsageExample collocation
mass mediachannels that reach very large audiencesThe mass media shape how the public sees events.
broadcastto transmit a programme by radio or televisionThe debate was broadcast live to millions.
coveragethe reporting of an event by the mediaThe disaster received round-the-clock coverage.
journalismthe activity of gathering and reporting newsInvestigative journalism exposed the scandal.
journalista person who researches and reports newsA seasoned journalist verifies every source.
correspondenta reporter who covers a region or topicThe foreign correspondent filed a report from the front.
headlinethe title of a news story in large printA sensational headline grabbed readers' attention.
outleta publication or channel that distributes newsSeveral news outlets ran the same story.
circulationthe number of copies a publication sellsPrint newspapers have seen circulation decline sharply.
readershipthe people who read a publicationThe magazine built a loyal readership.
viewershipthe audience that watches a programmeThe final drew record viewership.
audiencethe people who watch, read, or listen to contentStreaming platforms reach a global audience.
tabloida popular paper focused on sensational storiesThe tabloid press thrives on celebrity gossip.
broadsheeta serious newspaper with in-depth reportingThe broadsheet ran a detailed analysis of the budget.
sensationalismpresenting news to provoke excitement over accuracySensationalism distorts the public's sense of risk.
clickbaitcontent designed mainly to attract online clicksMisleading clickbait erodes trust in the site.
biasa tendency to favour one side unfairlyReaders accused the channel of political bias.
objectivityreporting facts without personal opinionGood journalism strives for objectivity.
impartialityfairness and refusal to take sidesPublic broadcasters are expected to show impartiality.
credibilitythe quality of being trusted and believedRepeated errors damaged the paper's credibility.
misinformationfalse information spread regardless of intentMisinformation spreads rapidly on social platforms.
disinformationfalse information spread deliberately to deceiveState-backed disinformation targeted the election.
fake newsfabricated stories presented as genuine reportingThe spread of fake news undermines informed debate.
propagandabiased information promoting a cause or viewThe regime flooded the airwaves with propaganda.
censorshipsuppression of content deemed objectionableOnline censorship limits access to dissenting views.
freedom of the pressthe right of media to report without controlFreedom of the press is vital to accountability.
watchdoga body or outlet that monitors abuses of powerThe press acts as a watchdog over government.
scrutinyclose and critical examinationOfficials came under intense media scrutiny.
exposéa report revealing wrongdoingThe exposé forced the minister to resign.
editorialan article giving a publication's opinionThe editorial called for urgent reform.
op-edan opinion piece by an outside writerShe wrote an op-ed defending the policy.
featurea longer article exploring a topic in depthThe weekend feature profiled young entrepreneurs.
publicitypublic attention given to a person or productThe stunt generated enormous publicity.
public relationsmanaging an organisation's public imageThe firm hired a public relations team after the crisis.
spinpresenting information in a favourable lightOfficials put a positive spin on the figures.
advertisingpromoting products or services to the publicDigital advertising now outspends print.
advertisementa paid public notice promoting somethingA clever advertisement can go viral overnight.
campaigna planned series of promotional activitiesThe campaign boosted sales by a third.
marketingactivities that promote and sell productsTargeted marketing reaches specific customer groups.
brandingcreating a distinct image for a product or firmStrong branding builds customer loyalty.
slogana short memorable phrase used in advertisingA catchy slogan lodges in the memory.
logoa symbol that identifies a brandThe redesigned logo modernised the brand.
endorsementpublic approval of a product by a known figureA celebrity endorsement lifted the product's profile.
sponsorshipfinancial support in return for promotionCorporate sponsorship funds many sporting events.
product placementfeaturing products within entertainment contentProduct placement blurs the line between film and advert.
consumerisma preoccupation with buying goods and servicesAdvertising fuels a culture of consumerism.
target audiencethe specific group a message aims to reachThe advert was tailored to a young target audience.
demographica group defined by shared characteristicsThe brand chased a wealthier demographic.
persuasionthe act of convincing someone to act or believeAdverts rely on subtle persuasion.
manipulationinfluencing people unfairly for one's own endsCritics accuse advertisers of manipulation.
exposurethe extent to which people see a messageRepeated exposure makes a brand familiar.
saturationthe point at which a market is flooded with adsAd saturation leaves consumers desensitised.
consumptionthe using up of goods, services, or contentMedia consumption has shifted to mobile devices.
influencera person who shapes others' choices onlineAn influencer can sway thousands of followers.
social mediaonline platforms for sharing content and contactSocial media has transformed how news spreads.
platforman online service hosting user contentEach platform favours a different kind of content.
viralspreading rapidly and widely onlineThe clip went viral within hours.
trendingcurrently popular and widely discussedThe hashtag was trending worldwide.
feeda stream of updated content on a platformAlgorithms decide what appears in your feed.
algorithmrules that decide which content users seeThe algorithm rewards engaging, emotive posts.
engagementhow much users interact with contentControversy drives up engagement.
echo chambera space where only like-minded views are heardOnline echo chambers harden opinions.
filter bubbleisolation from differing views by personalisationA filter bubble narrows what users encounter.
anonymitythe state of being unidentified onlineAnonymity can embolden abusive behaviour.
cyberbullyingharassing or threatening someone onlineSchools now run programmes against cyberbullying.
trollingposting provocative content to upset othersPersistent trolling drives users off platforms.
defamationdamaging someone's reputation with false claimsThe article led to a defamation lawsuit.
libela false, damaging statement in writingThe paper was sued for libel.
slandera false, damaging spoken statementHe threatened to sue for slander.
privacythe right to keep personal matters privateData collection raises serious privacy concerns.
surveillancemonitoring of people's activity or dataOnline surveillance tracks consumer behaviour.
data mininganalysing large data sets to find patternsData mining lets advertisers predict preferences.
regulationofficial rules controlling media conductCalls grow for tighter regulation of platforms.
accountabilitybeing answerable for published contentPlatforms face pressure for greater accountability.
moderationreviewing and removing harmful online contentContent moderation struggles to keep pace.
literacythe ability to evaluate media criticallyMedia literacy helps users spot false claims.
entertainmentcontent produced mainly to amuse or divertStreaming blurs news and entertainment.
documentarya factual film about real eventsThe documentary drew attention to the crisis.
broadcasteran organisation that transmits programmesA public broadcaster is funded by licence fees.
streamingwatching or listening to content in real time onlineStreaming services have reshaped viewing habits.
subscriptiona paid arrangement for ongoing access to contentThe paper moved to a subscription model.
paywalla barrier requiring payment to read contentA paywall limits free access to articles.
monetiseto earn revenue from content or a serviceCreators monetise videos through adverts.
revenueincome generated by a business or outletAdvertising revenue funds free content.
ratingsmeasures of how many people watch a programmeHigh ratings attract more advertisers.
circulation warfierce competition between papers for readersA circulation war pushed prices lower.
hypeintense, often exaggerated promotionThe film failed to live up to the hype.
backlasha strong negative public reactionThe advert sparked an immediate backlash.
boycottrefusing to buy from a firm as protestConsumers organised a boycott of the brand.
brand loyaltya customer's consistent preference for a brandRewards schemes reinforce brand loyalty.
word of mouthinformal personal recommendation of a productMuch of the growth came through word of mouth.
