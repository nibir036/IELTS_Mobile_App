Connecting Words & Linkers

Part 1 of the Academic Word List · grouped by function · use note · example · formality tag. 150 connectors.

1 · Adding information

Use these to pile on a further, supporting point.

furthermore
Adds a stronger, separate point to what you have just said.
The plan is costly. Furthermore, it is unlikely to work.
additionformal
moreover
Very similar to "furthermore"; adds an important extra point.
She is highly qualified; moreover, she has years of experience.
additionformal
in addition
Neutral way to add a further point or item.
In addition, the service is available around the clock.
additionneutral
additionally
Adverb form; adds another fact or point.
Additionally, the app works offline.
additionformal
what is more
Adds a point that strengthens the argument.
It is cheap, and what is more, it is reliable.
additionneutral
besides
Adds a point, often a decisive extra reason.
I don't want to go; besides, it's too late.
additionneutral
not only … but also
Links two linked points, emphasising both.
It is not only efficient but also affordable.
additionneutral
as well as
Adds an item alongside another.
He teaches maths as well as physics.
additionneutral
coupled with
Combines two factors into one effect.
Poor planning, coupled with bad weather, ruined the trip.
additionformal
on top of that
Informal way to add a further (often negative) point.
The flight was delayed, and on top of that, we lost a bag.
additioninformal

2 · Contrast & opposition

Use these to signal a difference or an opposing idea.

however
The standard linker for a contrasting point.
The idea is bold. However, it carries real risks.
contrastneutral
nevertheless
"In spite of that"; concedes a point then pushes on.
It was raining; nevertheless, the match went ahead.
contrastformal
nonetheless
Same meaning as "nevertheless"; slightly more formal.
The costs are high; nonetheless, the benefits justify them.
contrastformal
on the other hand
Introduces the opposite side of an argument.
Cars are convenient; on the other hand, they pollute.
contrastneutral
on the contrary
Rejects a previous statement and asserts the opposite.
It was not dull; on the contrary, it was gripping.
contrastformal
conversely
Introduces an idea that is the reverse of the last one.
Cities offer jobs; conversely, they can feel isolating.
contrastformal
in contrast
Highlights a clear difference between two things.
Sales rose in the city. In contrast, rural sales fell.
contrastformal
whereas
Directly compares two contrasting facts in one sentence.
He is cautious, whereas she takes risks.
contrastneutral
while
Contrasts two clauses (also means "during").
While some enjoy routine, others crave change.
contrastneutral
yet
Signals a surprising contrast; "but".
It is simple, yet remarkably effective.
contrastneutral
even so
"Despite that"; concedes then maintains a position.
The evidence is thin; even so, the idea is worth testing.
contrastneutral
despite / in spite of
Followed by a noun or -ing; shows something happens anyway.
Despite the cost, demand kept rising.
contrastformal
notwithstanding
Very formal "despite".
Notwithstanding the delays, the project succeeded.
contrastformal
although / even though
Introduces a concession clause.
Although it was late, they carried on.
contrastneutral
instead
Signals a replacement or alternative choice.
Don't give up; instead, try a new approach.
contrastneutral

3 · Cause & reason

Use these to explain why something happens.

because
The everyday linker giving a direct reason.
We stayed in because it was pouring.
causeneutral
since
Gives a reason, usually one already known.
Since funds were limited, we cut the budget.
causeneutral
as
Gives a reason, slightly more formal than "because".
As the roads were closed, we turned back.
causeneutral
due to
Followed by a noun; gives the cause of something.
The delay was due to heavy traffic.
causeformal
owing to
Formal "because of"; followed by a noun.
Owing to the storm, flights were grounded.
causeformal
on account of
Formal phrase introducing a cause.
The event was postponed on account of the weather.
causeformal
given that
"Considering the fact that"; sets up a reason.
Given that time is short, we must prioritise.
causeformal
in view of
"Because of"; introduces a reason for a decision.
In view of the risks, the plan was dropped.
causeformal
seeing that
Gives an obvious reason; slightly informal.
Seeing that it's late, let's finish here.
causeinformal

4 · Result & consequence

Use these to show the effect of something.

therefore
The standard linker introducing a logical result.
Demand rose; therefore, prices climbed.
resultformal
thus
Formal "as a result"; often introduces a consequence.
The data was flawed; thus, the findings are unreliable.
resultformal
hence
Formal "for this reason"; introduces a result.
He was unwell; hence his absence.
resultformal
consequently
Introduces a direct consequence.
She missed the train; consequently, she was late.
resultformal
as a result
Neutral way to show an outcome.
Costs fell, and as a result, profits grew.
resultneutral
as a consequence
Similar to "as a result"; slightly more formal.
The law changed; as a consequence, firms adapted.
resultformal
accordingly
"In a way that fits"; acts in response to a situation.
Rules changed, and staff behaved accordingly.
resultformal
as such
"For that reason"; links a status to its consequence.
He is a beginner and, as such, needs guidance.
resultformal
that is why
Neutral, conversational way to give a reason-result link.
It rained all week; that is why the trip was cancelled.
resultneutral

5 · Purpose

Use these to show the aim behind an action.

in order to
States the purpose of an action.
She studied hard in order to pass.
purposeneutral
so as to
Formal way to express purpose.
He left early so as to avoid the rush.
purposeformal
so that
Introduces a purpose clause with a verb.
Speak clearly so that everyone can follow.
purposeneutral
with a view to
Formal; followed by -ing, states an aim.
They met with a view to forming a partnership.
purposeformal
with the aim of
States a goal; followed by -ing.
The scheme was set up with the aim of cutting waste.
purposeformal

6 · Sequence & order

Use these to order points or stages in time.

firstly / secondly / thirdly
Orders a list of points in an argument.
Firstly, it is cheap; secondly, it is fast.
sequenceneutral
to begin with
Introduces the first point or stage.
To begin with, we need a clear plan.
sequenceneutral
subsequently
Formal "afterwards"; shows what came next.
He trained abroad and subsequently returned home.
sequenceformal
afterwards
"After that"; orders events in time.
We ate first and went out afterwards.
sequenceneutral
following this
Formal way to introduce the next stage.
Following this, the results were reviewed.
sequenceformal
meanwhile
Shows something happening at the same time.
He cooked; meanwhile, I set the table.
sequenceneutral
prior to
Formal "before"; followed by a noun or -ing.
Prior to the meeting, we read the report.
sequenceformal
eventually
"In the end, after some time."
After many tries, she eventually succeeded.
sequenceneutral
finally / lastly
Introduces the last point in a sequence.
Finally, we should consider the cost.
sequenceneutral

7 · Examples & illustration

Use these to introduce a supporting example.

for example / for instance
The standard way to introduce an example.
Many cities, for example Tokyo, face overcrowding.
exampleneutral
such as
Introduces examples within a sentence.
Skills such as patience are vital.
exampleneutral
namely
Introduces a specific detail just mentioned.
One factor stands out, namely cost.
exampleformal
to illustrate
Introduces an example to make a point clear.
To illustrate, consider the case of remote work.
exampleformal
in particular
Singles out one example for emphasis.
Young people, in particular, are affected.
exampleneutral
particularly / especially
Emphasises one case above others.
Costs rose, especially for housing.
exampleneutral

8 · Emphasis

Use these to stress or strengthen a point.

indeed
Confirms and strengthens a previous statement.
It was hard; indeed, it was the hardest year yet.
emphasisformal
in fact
Adds a surprising or stronger truth.
It wasn't dull; in fact, it was fascinating.
emphasisneutral
notably
Draws attention to a significant point.
Several countries, notably Japan, are ageing fast.
emphasisformal
above all
Marks the most important point.
Above all, honesty matters most.
emphasisneutral
undoubtedly
States something as beyond doubt.
Undoubtedly, education changes lives.
emphasisformal
significantly
Flags a point as important or meaningful.
Significantly, support rose among the young.
emphasisformal
it should be noted that
Formally flags a point the reader should register.
It should be noted that results may vary.
emphasisformal

9 · Comparison & similarity

Use these to show that two things are alike.

similarly
Shows a second case resembling the first.
Sales fell in the north; similarly, the south declined.
comparisonformal
likewise
"In the same way"; links two similar ideas.
She works hard; likewise, her team does.
comparisonformal
in the same way
Draws a parallel between two situations.
In the same way, habits shape our days.
comparisonneutral
equally
Signals two things are true to the same degree.
Rest matters; equally, so does exercise.
comparisonneutral
by the same token
"For the same reason"; extends a line of thought.
He values trust; by the same token, he hates lies.
comparisonformal
compared with / to
Sets two things side by side.
Compared with last year, costs are lower.
comparisonneutral

10 · Condition

Use these to show something depends on a condition.

provided that
"Only if"; sets a firm condition.
You may go provided that you finish first.
conditionformal
as long as
States a condition that must hold.
It works as long as you follow the steps.
conditionneutral
unless
"If not"; states the exception.
We'll leave at six unless it rains.
conditionneutral
in case
Prepares for a possible situation.
Take an umbrella in case it rains.
conditionneutral
on condition that
Formal; states a strict requirement.
He agreed on condition that his name be kept private.
conditionformal
even if
Shows something holds regardless of a condition.
I'll finish it even if it takes all night.
conditionneutral
otherwise
States what happens if a condition is not met.
Hurry, otherwise we'll miss it.
conditionneutral

11 · Concession

Use these to admit a point before countering it.

admittedly
Concedes a point before you argue against it.
Admittedly, the plan has flaws, but it is a start.
concessionformal
granted
Accepts an opposing point briefly.
Granted, it is expensive, yet it lasts for years.
concessionneutral
it is true that
Concedes a fact before a "but".
It is true that risks exist, but they are manageable.
concessionneutral
despite the fact that
Formal concession followed by a clause.
Despite the fact that it rained, turnout was high.
concessionformal
while it is true that
Balances a concession against your own view.
While it is true that cars pollute, they aid mobility.
concessionformal

12 · Clarification & restatement

Use these to restate an idea more clearly.

in other words
Restates an idea more simply.
It is non-refundable; in other words, you lose the money.
clarifyneutral
that is to say
Introduces a clearer restatement.
He is frugal, that is to say, careful with money.
clarifyformal
to put it another way
Rephrases a point from a new angle.
To put it another way, quality beats quantity.
clarifyneutral
to clarify
Signals you are making a point clearer.
To clarify, the deadline is Friday, not Monday.
clarifyneutral

13 · Generalisation

Use these to state what is usually or broadly true.

in general
States what is true most of the time.
In general, prices rise over time.
generaliseneutral
on the whole
Gives an overall judgement.
On the whole, the response was positive.
generaliseneutral
broadly speaking
Makes a general statement without exact detail.
Broadly speaking, the two plans are alike.
generaliseneutral
as a rule
States a usual pattern.
As a rule, I avoid caffeine at night.
generaliseneutral
by and large
"Mostly"; gives a broad verdict.
By and large, the reforms worked.
generaliseneutral
for the most part
"In most cases"; allows for exceptions.
For the most part, the service is reliable.
generaliseneutral

14 · Conclusion & summary

Use these to close an argument or sum up.

in conclusion
Signals the final paragraph of an essay.
In conclusion, the benefits outweigh the costs.
conclusionformal
to conclude
Introduces a closing statement.
To conclude, change is both needed and overdue.
conclusionformal
in summary
Introduces a brief recap.
In summary, three factors drive the trend.
conclusionformal
to sum up
Neutral way to summarise.
To sum up, planning saves both time and money.
conclusionneutral
on balance
Gives a final verdict after weighing both sides.
On balance, the advantages win out.
conclusionneutral
all in all
"Considering everything"; sums up overall.
All in all, it was a worthwhile experience.
conclusionneutral
all things considered
Gives a judgement after weighing all factors.
All things considered, the plan is sound.
conclusionneutral
ultimately
Points to the final, most important outcome.
Ultimately, the decision rests with voters.
conclusionformal
