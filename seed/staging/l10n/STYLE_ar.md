# Arabic style guide for app content (IELTS AI by nextED)

Same idea as the Bangla version (STYLE_bn.md): **Arabic explanation, English examples**. The reader is an IELTS
student in the Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait), often weak in English. Write EASY Modern Standard
Arabic (فصحى مبسطة) — clear, short sentences a teacher would use in class, no heavy literary style. The app shows
Arabic right-to-left.

## Voice
- Address the student directly and neutrally ("ابحث عن…", "لاحظ أن…", "عليك أن…"); short clear sentences.
- Re-say the idea naturally in Arabic; never word-for-word.
- Arabic punctuation in Arabic sentences: ، for commas, ؛ for semicolons, ؟ for questions; "." as the full stop.

## Keep in English (never translate)
- IELTS / exam terms students see on the paper or use in class: passage, paragraph, question, option, keyword,
  paraphrase, synonym, distractor, trap, skim, scan, True / False / Not Given, Yes / No / Not Given, Matching
  Headings, Summary Completion, examiner, band, evidence, topic sentence, word limit, NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS,
  grammar terms (subject, verb, article, tense, clause …), Task 1 / Task 2, Part 1 / 2 / 3, cue card, fluency …
  Write them in Latin letters as separate words inside the Arabic sentence ("في الـ passage"، "الـ keyword"،
  "كل option"). Never glue Arabic letters onto a Latin word and never transliterate the term into Arabic letters.
  The first time a term appears in a block you may add a short Arabic gloss in brackets: "paraphrase (إعادة صياغة)".
- Everything quoted from the passage, the question text, option text, answers, example sentences, model answers
  and paragraph letters: keep them in English exactly as given, inside the same quotation marks.
- Option letters (A, B, C…), paragraph letters, question numbers, years and figures stay as they are, in Western
  digits (0–9). Use Western digits in your own counts too (٢ is not used): "3 خطوات"، "10 دقائق".
- Brand names: IELTS AI by nextED.

## Example of the target style
> يقول الـ passage إن السطح كان "wasted space"، وهذه هي إعادة صياغة (paraphrase) لعبارة "an area of little value"
> في الـ option B. أما D فيتحدث عن الوقت الحاضر وليس عن twentieth century. ويذكر الـ passage عمال الصيانة، لكنه لا
> يقول أبدًا إن الأسطح كانت خطرة (C).

## Bangla-specific parts
Some lessons were written for Bangla speakers (e.g. "Bangla speakers often say…", Bangla example sentences in
Bengali script, "Bangla logic" boxes). Translate them faithfully: "Bangla" stays the language being described
("البنغالية"، "المتحدثون بالبنغالية"), every Bengali-script example is kept exactly as it is, and only the
explanation around it is translated. Exception: where "Bangla" simply means the student's own mother tongue — "restate the task in your own words (in Bangla)", "think in Bangla first", "explanations are in Bangla", "its Bangla meaning" next to a word list now glossed in Arabic — write Arabic instead. Do not rewrite them for Arabic speakers.

## Accuracy
- Keep every fact and every reason of the English original; add nothing new, drop nothing.
- If the English says why a wrong option is wrong, the Arabic must say it too.
- Keep **bold** markers around the matching words.
